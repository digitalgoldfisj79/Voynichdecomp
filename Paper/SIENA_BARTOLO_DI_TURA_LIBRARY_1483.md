**Status:** source-reconstructed catalogue, 2026-08-30  
**Person:** Bartolo di Tura di Bandino (Bandini), Sienese physician

**Modern synthesis:** Donatella Nebbiai-Dalla Guarda, “Livres, patrimoines, profession : les bibliothèques de quelques médecins en Italie (XIVe-XVe siècles),” *Publications de l’École française de Rome* 238 (1997), pp. 385–441.

## Counting note

Mazzi prints Bartolo's books inside a room-by-room estate inventory whose numbering continues through no. 221 and includes furniture, instruments, household objects, loose documents and other non-library material. The later scholarly total of **174 library items** can be reconstructed exactly from the book-bearing blocks:

- inventory nos. **1–121**: 121 entries in Bartolo's ground-floor professional *studio*;
- inventory nos. **158–184 and 193–195**: 30 entries in the upstairs/private-study material;
- inventory nos. **196–218**: 23 book/account entries kept in a sack with the Gesuati.

Total: **121 + 30 + 23 = 174 catalogue entries**.

This is a catalogue-entry count, not necessarily a count of individual physical codices. Several entries explicitly contain multiple books, treatises or quires (for example no. 118 contains seven law books; no. 181 contains two blank books; no. 211 contains two bastardelli). Conversely, some damaged quires may not have been bound volumes.

## Evidential discipline

**Known:** the 1483 inventory records the following material in Bartolo's estate/family holdings.  
**Not known:** that every item was already owned by Bartolo in the Voynich production window c.1420–1450. The inventory post-dates his death by six years and his career continued well after 1450. Individual codices must be dated/provenanced before any title is used as proof of pre-1450 Sienese access.

The “Normalized identification” column follows Mazzi where possible and modernizes obvious author/work names. Where Mazzi or the inventory is uncertain, that uncertainty is retained.

## High-value intersections with the Voynich reference-library question

The inventory is substantially more useful than the generic statement that Bartolo owned a large medical library. In one named Sienese physician's working collection it records several exact or near-exact source families independently relevant to the Voynich comparison:

- **Botany / simples / pharmacology:** Dioscorides *Materia medica* (estate 62); *Circa instans* (100); *Aggregator / De simplicibus* (28, 31); Serapion *De simplicibus* (48, 80) and *Breviarium* (93); *Tacuinum sanitatis* (54); Platearius / *Antidotarium Nicolai* material (65, 85); Mesue (90, 102); Avicenna throughout.
- **Astronomy / cosmology / mathematical astrology:** Alfonsine Tables (58); an astrology book “con molto albaco” (83); a *scriptum* on the Sphere (106); another *Spera* (113).
- **Anatomical / practical medicine:** surgery texts including Guglielmo da Saliceto (7), Lanfranc (79), an anonymous surgery book (108), and two medical books explicitly described as containing a human figure showing veins, nerves and joints (120–121).
- **Cross-disciplinary learned culture:** Aristotle, Albertus Magnus, Averroes, Thomas Aquinas, Paul of Venice, John of Jandun, Richard Swineshead, Pseudo-Aristotle *Secretum secretorum*, classical geography (Strabo), rhetoric and humanist material.

### What it does *not* establish

No title in the present reconstruction securely identifies:
- a *De Balneis* witness;
- Taccola's works;
- Kyeser/*Bellifortis*;
- Goro Dati's *La Sfera* specifically (the two “Sphere” entries are generic/anonymous as inventoried);
- Oresme.

Those traditions must remain separate access questions.

## Historical implication

This inventory is evidence for a **named Sienese medical practitioner with a genuinely multidisciplinary working reference library**, not merely for the abstract availability of medical books in Siena. It materially strengthens the “reference-library ecology” part of the Siena hypothesis, especially the herbal/pharmaceutical + cosmological/astrological conjunction. It does not by itself establish that the same titles were accessible in Siena by c.1450; the next useful operation is codex-level dating/provenance of the high-value entries rather than further thematic expansion.

## Source-control notes

1. Preserve Mazzi's estate numbering in all downstream graph/database work.
2. Do not silently convert uncertain inventory readings into secure author/work identifications.
3. Do not infer pre-1450 possession from the 1483 inventory without independent codicological or documentary evidence.
4. Treat “174” as the reconstructed count of library catalogue entries, not automatically 174 physical bound volumes.
