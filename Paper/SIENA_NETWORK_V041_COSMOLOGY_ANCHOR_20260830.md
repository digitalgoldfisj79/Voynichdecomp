## Question
Can a relevant illustrated/scientific manuscript or visual scientific tradition be tied directly into the same named Siena manuscript-production network before c.1450?

## Result
**Yes for cosmology, with an exact-exemplar caveat.**

### Named chain
1. In Siena on 5 September 1417, fra Niccolò Galgani Arrigucci of San Domenico paid a painter called Giovanni for gilding and probably illuminating a Book of Hours for Anna, wife of the Milanese/Pavia jurist Cristoforo Castiglioni. Scholarship identifies this as the earliest documentary evidence for Giovanni di Paolo.
2. Galgani's surviving `Memoriale` independently documents active manuscript manufacture and circulation: paper/parchment, copyists, illuminators, binding, professional German scribes, physicians, and scientific/natural-philosophy books.
3. Giovanni di Paolo remained a Sienese painter/illuminator. British Library Yates Thompson MS 36 has its `Paradiso` (ff.129–190v) illuminated by Giovanni c.1450. The Warburg Institute localises the manuscript to Siena, c.1444–1450.
4. The Yates Thompson `Paradiso` is not merely devotional imagery. BL identifies f.130r as the hierarchy of being/heaven's nine spheres and f.132r as Beatrice explaining scientific theories including the appearance of the Moon.
5. Independently, Giovanni's 1445 `Creation of the World and Expulsion from Paradise`, made for the Guelfi chapel at San Domenico, Siena, depicts the geocentric universe, four elements, planets and zodiac. Laurinda Dixon (`Art Bulletin` 67, 1985, 604–613; DOI 10.1080/00043079.1985.10788294) traces the source of its geocentric conception to the biblical/Aristotelian synthesis documented in Johannes Sacrobosco's `Sphera mundi`.

Thus a person already demonstrably inside Galgani's Siena manuscript-production network is subsequently documented producing an illustrated manuscript with substantial cosmological/scientific content in Siena and, independently, a securely Sienese cosmological image tied by scholarship to the Sacrobosco tradition.

## Nearby manuscript evidence
Siena BCI L.IV.29 (`Sie3`) is a paper manuscript of the second quarter of the fifteenth century containing vernacular Sacrobosco plus Goro Dati's `La Sfera`; diagrams are present. This is relevant corroborating ecology, but no evidence currently proves that Giovanni di Paolo used L.IV.29 or that this particular codex was already in Siena when he worked.

## Guardrails
- We have **not** identified Giovanni's exact Sacrobosco exemplar.
- We do **not** claim Giovanni used BCI L.IV.29.
- Yates Thompson 36 is a literary manuscript with substantial scientific/cosmological imagery, not a scientific textbook.
- This closes a named production-chain anchor for **cosmology**, not for the herbal source family.
- It does not identify the Voynich maker or prove Siena provenance for the Voynich manuscript.
- The failed Taccola/Q13 visual programme remains failed and unchanged.

## Verdict
`VERIFIED_WITH_EXEMPLAR_CAVEAT`

The residual question is no longer whether the Siena production network could generate illustrated scientific/cosmological material. It demonstrably could. The remaining high-value question is whether an exact scientific exemplar—or an equivalent herbal manuscript—can be tied to a named member of that network.
