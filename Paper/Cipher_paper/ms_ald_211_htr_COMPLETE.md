## NOTES ON THE MANUSCRIPT

### Identification
This is a version of the **Pseudo-Apuleius Herbarius** tradition combined with elements 
possibly from the **Macer Floridus** and vernacular Italian herbal additions. The numbered 
herb entries (S-series, then numbered 49, 70-75, q0-q8) suggest a compiled text drawing 
on multiple sources.

### Language characteristics
- Standard medieval pharmaceutical Latin with strong Italian vernacular influence
- Formula: "Ad [condition]. Accipe [ingredient] de ista herba..."
- Consistent use of "sanabitur/sanatur" outcome formula
- "probatum" (proven) attestation appearing frequently
- Vernacular intrusions: "erba corigola", "lauiztu sento", "zinzibiz" 
- Some entries show Dioscoridean attribution ("Dyascozides dixit")

### Relevance to VMS research
- 14th-century N. Italian pharmaceutical Latin herbal
- Recipe structure: condition → ingredient → preparation → administration → outcome
- Same textual tradition as Circa Instans but from the Pseudo-Apuleius branch
- Herbal illustrations with synonym lists (multilingual plant names)
- Production context: Pavia / N. Italy, parchment, iron-gall ink + rubrication

## ANALYSIS: Significance for VMS research

### Source text identification
This batch CONFIRMS that Ms.Ald.211 is a **composite manuscript** containing:
1. **Pseudo-Apuleius Herbarius** (batch 1 folios — S-numbered and numbered entries)
2. **Circa Instans / Platearius tradition** (this batch — explicit "ut dixit Platearius" attributions)
3. **Vernacular additions** and a **medical glossary**

### Platearius attributions (verbatim from manuscript):
- "Feniculum ut dixit Platearius" (fennel)
- "Menta ut dixit Platearius" (mint)
- "Marubium ut dixit Platearius" (horehound)
- "Petrosilium ut dixit Platearius" (parsley)
- "Herba papauis ut dixit Platearius" (poppy)

### Function word patterns in the pharmaceutical Latin
Common FWs appearing in these folios:
- **et** (extremely frequent — conjunction)
- **in** (preposition — "in aqua", "in uino", "in cibum")
- **cum** (preposition — "cum melle", "cum uino", "cum aqua")
- **de** (preposition — "de puluere", "de ista herba")
- **ad** (preposition — "ad dolorem", "ad sanandum")
- **que/quod** (relative — "que est domestica", "quod decoctum sit")
- **uel** (disjunction — "uel radiy", "uel puluis")
- **est** (copula — "herba est calida et sicca")
- **habet** (verb — "uim habet")
- **Item** (discourse marker — extremely frequent, starts new indications)
- **contra** (preposition — "contra opilatiaem", "contra lapidem")
- **supra/super** (preposition — "supra dentem", "sup morsum")
- **postea** (temporal — "postea pone", "postea fiacent")

### Recipe formula structure (typical pattern):
[HERB NAME] ut dixit Platearius, herba est [QUALITY] et [QUALITY].
[DESCRIPTION of parts and properties]
¶ Contra [CONDITION], [PREPARATION] cum [VEHICLE] [ADMINISTRATION].
¶ Item ad [CONDITION], [PREPARATION]... et sanaf/sanabitur.

### Eufraxia entry (54958) — notable length
The Eufraxia/luminella entry is unusually long and detailed, describing:
- Habitat (mountain tops, rocky places, among hay)
- Collection method
- Preparation (with white wine, on empty stomach, or as electuary with honey)
- Dosage regimen (40 days)
- Claimed efficacy for vision restoration
- Wine infusion method (eufraxiated wine)
- Personal testimonial attestation

### Medical glossary (54964, bottom)
The glossary at the bottom of 54964 provides Latin medical terminology with 
vernacular/simplified definitions — typical of a student/practitioner reference tool.
This further supports the manuscript's function as a practical medical reference
rather than a display/library copy.
