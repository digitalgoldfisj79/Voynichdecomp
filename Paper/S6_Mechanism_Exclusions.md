# S6: Formal Mechanism Exclusions

> **Source code:** `src/p70c_connect.py` (autocorrelation, MI calculations)  
> **Data:** `data/enriched_records.pkl`


## S6.1 Overview

Section 4.4 of the main paper excludes four classes of production mechanism. This supplement provides the formal arguments and supporting data for each exclusion.

## S6.2 Monoalphabetic Substitution

**Claim**: Simple substitution over natural-language prose is excluded.

**Argument**: Monoalphabetic substitution is a bijective character mapping. It preserves all distributional properties of the source text, including word-length autocorrelation. The VMS has positive length AC (AC(1) = +0.138). Natural-language prose in the candidate source languages shows negative or near-zero AC:

| Language | Corpus | AC(1) |
|----------|--------|-------|
| Latin (Tacuinum) | Medical | −0.023 |
| Italian (Riccardiana) | Herbal | −0.018 |
| German (Konrad) | Botanical | +0.012 |
| English (Chaucer) | Literary | −0.031 |

Since monoalphabetic substitution preserves AC, and no tested source language has AC ≥ +0.10, no monoalphabetic cipher over these languages can produce the VMS AC value.

**Scope**: This excludes simple substitution only. Verbose homophonic substitution, nomenclator systems, and other non-bijective mappings are not excluded by this argument alone.

## S6.3 Word-Level Encryption

**Claim**: Any word-boundary-preserving cipher applied to natural language is excluded.

**Argument**: Word-boundary-preserving encryption maps source words to cipher words, preserving word boundaries. Under any such mapping, the mutual information between adjacent cipher words equals the MI between adjacent source words (since MI is invariant under bijective transformation of either variable, and even under many-to-one mappings, MI cannot increase).

Natural language word-to-word MI₁ ranges from 3–5 bits. VMS MI₁ = 0.45 bits, an order of magnitude below. Decomposition shows 98% derives from vocabulary frequencies (a trivial correlate of word identity) rather than sequential structure.

**Caveat**: This does not exclude verbose homophonic systems where one source word maps to multiple cipher words (breaking the word-boundary preservation). The Naibbe cipher (Greshko 2025) demonstrates that such systems can replicate many VMS word-level statistics, but fail on AC, PFG, and gallows selection grammar.

## S6.4 Independent Word Generation (IID)

**Claim**: Words are not generated independently, even from position-specific pools.

**Argument**: Under independent word generation (IID sampling), the expected magnitude of length autocorrelation is bounded by the variance structure of the position-specific length distributions. We prove:

For a sequence of N words drawn IID from position-specific pools with length variance σ²ₚ at position p:

    E[|AC(1)|] ≤ max_p(σ²ₚ) / (N × global_variance)

For VMS parameters (N = 37,465, σ² ≈ 2.1), this bound is approximately 0.025.

Simulation with 10,000 replications confirms: no replication exceeds |AC| = 0.028. The observed value (0.138) exceeds the bound by a factor of 5.5 (p < 10⁻⁴).

**The specific coupling mechanism**: The suffix-to-prefix transition grammar (§3.2 of main paper) identifies the pathway. Each word's suffix constrains the following word's prefix through the 8×8 transition matrix, and prefix constrains word length. The coupling propagates through PGCS slot structure, not through direct length dependence.

## S6.5 Cardan Grille (Rugg 2004)

**Claim**: Grille-based generation is excluded by morphological precision.

**Argument**: The Cardan grille hypothesis proposes that VMS text was generated by sliding a grille over a table of syllables. We tested this by implementing grille-based generators with parameters matching Rugg's published specification.

| Generator | PGCS violation rate | VMS violation rate |
|-----------|--------------------|--------------------|
| Rugg grille (original) | 8.4% | 0.0% |
| Rugg grille (optimised) | 6.7% | 0.0% |
| VMS self-consistency | 0.0% | 0.0% |

The VMS is more morphologically constrained than grille systems can achieve without explicit morphological knowledge. Grille generation produces 6.7–8.4% finite-state violations against the P70 grammar, while the VMS produces zero.

**Additional failures**:
- Grille generators produce flat position-frequency gradients (PFG ≈ −2 vs VMS −41.2)
- Grille generators lack suffix-to-prefix transitions (MI ≈ 0 vs VMS 0.757 bits)
- Grille generators cannot produce the autocorrelation signal (AC ≈ 0 vs VMS +0.138)

## S6.6 Summary: The Constraint Space

Any admissible production mechanism must simultaneously satisfy all constraints listed in Table 2 of the main paper. No tested single mechanism satisfies more than 67% of character-level and word-level constraints jointly. The PGCS stochastic generator, explicitly designed to satisfy all structural constraints without semantic content, achieves 83.7% of BG benchmarks — approaching the 86% self-consistency ceiling but failing on the position-frequency gradient.

The narrow gap between structural ceiling (83.7%) and self-consistency ceiling (86%) defines the available space for genuine content encoding. The information budget's 71.1% unexplained entropy is the upper bound on content; the 2.3% gap between ceilings is the lower bound on structure-independent variation.
