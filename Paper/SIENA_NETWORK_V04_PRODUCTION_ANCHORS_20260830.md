## Pre-specified test

Before this search, three evidential anchors were specified as the plausible surviving evidence capable of separating Siena from Padua/Pavia:

1. A surviving person/household/institution demonstrably handling several independently relevant knowledge/production strands.
2. A securely Sienese c.1420–1450 physical manuscript analogue showing relevant production behaviour rather than merely similar imagery.
3. A named cross-domain book-production chain linking scribes/illuminators/bookmakers to scientific/medical/foreign networks.

## Anchor 1: Niccolò Galgani Memoriale

Archivio di Stato di Siena, Patrimonio Resti, San Domenico 2175. Autograph working notebook, entries 1399–1423.

The same surviving transactional source documents:

- book lending, acquisition, sale, pledging and commissioning;
- purchases of paper/parchment and binding/copying costs;
- professional German scribes, including Henricus Teutonicus and Conradus Richardi de Alfordia;
- copying of `mathematica` and Albertus Magnus `De caelo et mundo`;
- book loans in natural philosophy to physicians including Ugo Benzi;
- astronomy/cosmology/natural-philosophy texts in the circulation network;
- Milan/Pavia jurists of the Castiglione family resident in Siena;
- Giovanni di Paolo's documented 1417 illumination of a Book of Hours for Anna Castiglione;
- Jacopo della Quercia commissioned through the same Galgani/Castiglione social network in 1418;
- Galgani's private coded/secret notation within the Memoriale;
- a 1423 therapeutic-bath journey (`balneum Maciareti`; exact location unresolved).

Guardrails: no illustrated herbal has yet been identified in the Memoriale; the unspecified `mathematica` is not identified with a particular work; Galgani's cipher is not Voynichese; `balneum Maciareti` is not equated with Petriolo; Galgani died in 1424 and is not proposed as the Voynich author.

## Anchor 2: Nicolò di Giovanni di Francesco di Ventura

A Sienese `pizzicaiuolo` — a seller of goods including wax, paper and colours/pigments — and member of the Sienese painters' guild.

BCI A.IV.5, *La sconfitta di Monte Aperto*, is securely Sienese and dated 1442–1443. Nicolò personally wrote the text and supplied the manuscript's 39 pen-and-watercolour/gouache illustrations. The manuscript is on paper and its text/image programme was planned as an integrated book. Scholarship indicates that older textual and visual models were reworked in its production.

In 1447 Nicolò was rector of the Arte dei Pizzicaiuoli, and the contract commissioning Giovanni di Paolo for the guild's Santa Maria della Scala altarpiece was made in Nicolò's Campo Fori shop.

This is a production-behaviour analogue only. It is not a claim of visual relationship with the Voynich manuscript.

## Anchor 3: named production chains

Verified chains now include:

- Henricus Teutonicus → Niccolò Galgani → Ugo Benzi.
- Henricus Teutonicus → Niccolò Galgani → Giovanni di Paolo.
- Cristoforo/Anna Castiglione (Pavia/Milan legal milieu) → Niccolò Galgani → Giovanni di Paolo.
- Nicolò di Giovanni Ventura → Giovanni di Paolo → Santa Maria della Scala.

The Galgani-to-Jacopo edge is verified in 1418; the Jacopo-to-Taccola edge is later. No simultaneous three-person meeting is inferred.

## Matched benchmark: Padua and Pavia/Milan

Padua is not a straw control. Pietro Donato's 1443–1445 inventory lists 358 books; he actively hunted manuscripts and commissioned internationally produced/illuminated codices. Padua also has hard physical anchors including the Carrara Herbal, Vat. lat. 4082 (copied at Padua 1401–1402 by Petrus de Fita), and Canon. Misc. 554 (Padua, 20 Feb 1435, Candus).

Pavia/Milan is equally serious. The 1426 Visconti library inventory contains 988 articles and is supplemented by documentation on acquisition and loans. Hard production anchors include the Milanese *Historia Plantarum* c.1395–1400 and BnF lat. 7258, restored/partly recopied at Pavia c.1390 by the German scribe Armannus de Alemania.

Therefore v0.4 does **not** conclude that Siena is unique in possessing manuscript-production anchors. Its current comparative advantage is qualitative: an unusually broad set of relevant functions is documented as actual transactions inside a compact working network, rather than primarily as holdings or elite patronage.
