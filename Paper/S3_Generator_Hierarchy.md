# S3: Generator Hierarchy — Specification, Scoring, and Results

> **Source code:** `reproduce_all.py` (complete reproduction script), `score_85_metrics.py` (90-metric scoring suite)  
> **Data:** `data/enriched_records.pkl`, `data/p70c_full_spec_v1.json`


## S3.1 The Six-Tier Hierarchy

All six generators implement the same core production loop — copy a recent word, modify a recent word, or create a new word from a ledger — differing only in the structural constraints governing the ledger and the modification rules. This isolates the effect of grammar from the effect of the production mechanism.

### Tier 1: Character Bigram (cf. Timm and Schinner 2020)

Implements character-level self-citation. A character bigram model is trained on f1r seed tokens (the manuscript's first page). Each new token is generated character-by-character: select a start character from the observed distribution, then follow the character bigram chain until an end marker or maximum length (12 characters) is reached. No word-level structure, no slot grammar, no positional conditioning.

This tier is the minimal baseline: it captures only character co-occurrence patterns from the seed vocabulary.

### Tier 2: Scribal Ductus

Implements calligraphic-group-level generation based on pen-stroke categories. Characters are grouped into four ductus classes reflecting 15th-century calligraphic practice: bench (*ch*, *sh*, *e*, *ee*, *eee*), gallows (*k*, *t*, *f*, *p*), loop (*o*, *a*, *y*), and ligature (*d*, *s*, *r*, *l*, *n*, *q*, *i*). A group-to-group bigram model is trained on the full corpus. Each token is generated by following group transitions and then selecting a character exemplar within each group. No PGCS slot structure, no word-level constraints.

This tier captures calligraphic habit: the structural regularities that emerge from pen-stroke sequencing without morphological awareness.

### Tier 3: P70C Single Ledger

The first grammar-enabled tier. Production draws from the PGCS constrained quad inventory (6,750 entries from `p70c_full_spec_v1.json`) using frequency-weighted sampling. The production loop operates at the slot level: copy (20%), modify one slot (50%), or create from the ledger (30%). Modification replaces a single PGCS slot (prefix, gallows, core, or suffix-family) with a value drawn from the corpus-wide marginal distribution for that slot. Token reconstruction uses the slot-to-token lookup table, with a 13% novelty rate producing new character sequences from slot combinations.

This tier introduces PGCS slot architecture but uses a single global ledger with no section or positional conditioning.

### Tier 4: P70C Dual A/B

Extends Tier 3 with Currier A/B vocabulary differentiation. The corpus is split into Currier A sections (Herbal-A, Pharmaceutical, Astronomical, Rosettes) and Currier B sections (Herbal-B, Stars, Balneological, Cosmological, Zodiac). Each section's tokens are generated using a blended entry probability: 70% global quad frequencies + 30% Currier-specific slot marginals (prefix, gallows, core, suffix-family distributions weighted by section membership). Section proportions match the VMS corpus distribution.

This tier tests whether Currier A/B differentiation — the oldest observed vocabulary split in the manuscript — adds statistical fidelity beyond global PGCS structure.

### Tier 5: P70C Section-Profiled

Extends Tier 4 with per-section parameterisation. Each of nine manuscript sections receives its own concentration parameter (controlling how sharply the section-specific slot distributions are weighted relative to the global distribution), copy rate, and flat-creation rate:

| Section | Concentration | Copy rate | Create rate |
|---------|--------------|-----------|-------------|
| Herbal-A | 1.0 | 0.20 | 0.40 |
| Herbal-B | 1.0 | 0.20 | 0.40 |
| Stars | 1.2 | 0.20 | 0.35 |
| Balneological | 1.5 | 0.30 | 0.25 |
| Pharmaceutical | 1.0 | 0.20 | 0.45 |
| Astronomical | 1.1 | 0.20 | 0.40 |
| Cosmological | 1.1 | 0.20 | 0.35 |
| Zodiac | 1.1 | 0.20 | 0.35 |
| Rosettes | 1.1 | 0.20 | 0.40 |

Entry probabilities blend 60% global + 40% section-weighted. The Balneological section receives the highest concentration (1.5) and copy rate (0.30), reflecting its distinctive vocabulary profile.

### Tier 6: Combined (Section + Folio Copy Pool)

The most specified tier. Extends Tier 5 with a folio-restricted copy pool: the copy operation (rate 0.35) draws only from the most recent 350 tokens (approximately one folio's worth), simulating a scribe who copies from the current page rather than from the entire manuscript. The modify rate is 0.40 (folio-restricted buffer of 200 tokens), and the create rate is 0.25.

This tier is designed to maximise word-length autocorrelation through local copying while maintaining section-specific vocabulary profiles. As documented in §4.4 of the main paper, this creates the impossibility triple: local copying raises AC but simultaneously raises repetition rate and depresses lexical diversity.


## S3.2 The 90-Metric Scoring Suite

Generators are scored against 90 distributional metrics covering thirteen domains:

| Domain | Metrics | Examples |
|--------|---------|---------|
| Word Length | 7 | mean, std, skew, unique word length stats, autocorrelation |
| Entropy Hierarchy | 7 | H0, H1, H2 conditional, h2/h3 joint and conditional |
| Character Distribution | 4 | evenness, redundancy, Simpson D, Yule K |
| Digraph/Trigraph | 3 | unique digraphs, coverage, unique trigraphs |
| TTR / Lexical Diversity | 13 | TTR, RTTR, CTTR, log TTR, Maas, Uber, Brunet, MSTTR, MATTR at 25/50/100 |
| Hapax and Frequency | 10 | hapax ratios, dis ratios, Sichel S, frequency spectrum |
| Frequency Concentration | 2 | top-10 share, top-50 share |
| Lexical Richness | 2 | Yule K (word), Honoré R |
| Autocorrelation | 4 | word length, word frequency, TTR-25, hapax-25 |
| BG Subsampling — Word | 14 | unique, change, distribution, bias (mean/std/skew) |
| BG Subsampling — Character | 10 | distribution, bias, n-gram distribution |
| Counts | 7 | unique/repeated/tripled words and characters, unique n-grams |
| Global | 7 | entropy, compression, Zipf, Zipf alpha, Zipf R², flipped pairs, Heaps beta |

The 15 core metrics (a subset used for rapid screening) are: TTR, H1 unigram, H2 Markov conditional, top-10 share, Zipf alpha, Zipf R², Heaps beta, word length mean, word length std, hapax ratio (types), repeated words, MATTR-25, MATTR-50, MATTR-100, and word length autocorrelation.

Scoring is binary: a metric passes if the generator's median value across 10 seeds falls within a fixed per-metric tolerance of the VMS baseline value. Tolerances are calibrated to the expected variation across manuscript partitions (split-half analysis) and are documented in the TOLERANCES dictionary of `score_85_metrics.py`. Representative values:

| Metric | VMS value | Tolerance | Rationale |
|--------|-----------|-----------|-----------|
| wordlen_mean | 4.984 | ±0.50 | ~10% of value |
| wordlen_autocorr | 0.160 | ±0.05 | Tight: key discriminator |
| H2_markov_cond | 2.342 | ±0.35 | BG benchmark range |
| ttr | 0.214 | ±0.03 | ~14% of value |
| mattr_25 | 0.919 | ±0.05 | ~5% of value |
| repeated_words | 0.008 | ±0.005 | Tight: impossibility metric |
| zipf_r2 | 0.915 | ±0.05 | Standard fit quality |

Full tolerances for all 90 metrics are in the source code. Each generator is run with 10 independent seeds (42–51); the median metric value across seeds is used for scoring.


## S3.3 Results

### Tier-by-Tier Scores

| Tier | Generator | Core 15 | Full 90 | Regime |
|------|-----------|---------|---------|--------|
| 1 | Bigram | 8/15 | 50/90 | No grammar |
| 2 | Scribal | 5/15 | 37/90 | No grammar |
| 3 | P70C Single | 9/15 | 64/90 | With grammar |
| 4 | Dual A/B | 7/15 | 58/90 | With grammar |
| 5 | Section | 10/15 | 67/90 | With grammar |
| 6 | Combined | 10/15 | 66/90 | With grammar |

*Note: Exact counts vary slightly across seed sets. Values shown are from the 10-seed median. Complete metric-by-metric results are in S3.4.*

The results separate cleanly into two regimes. Tiers 1–2 (no grammar) produce 37 to 50 passes. Tiers 3–6 (with grammar) produce 58 to 67. The jump from Tier 2 to Tier 3, when PGCS slot grammar is first introduced, is the largest single increment in the hierarchy. Within each regime, additional specification yields diminishing and non-monotonic returns: Tier 5 (Section-Profiled) achieves the highest score (67/90) while the more complex Tier 6 (Combined) scores 66/90, suggesting that folio-restricted copying slightly overfits local structure at the expense of global metrics. Similarly, Tier 1 (Bigram) outperforms Tier 2 (Scribal Ductus) at 50 vs 37, because bigram chains implicitly capture character-level regularities that the calligraphic-group model disrupts.

### Generator Ceiling

The best generator (Tier 5, Section-Profiled) approaches but does not reach the VMS's self-consistency across manuscript partitions (86%, measured by split-half metric agreement). The gap between the structural ceiling (67/90 = 74%) and the self-consistency ceiling (86%) represents genuine lexical decisions that no structural constraint captures.

When evaluated against the Bowern-Gaskell 42-metric benchmark subset specifically, the best tier (Section-Profiled) achieves 37/42 (88%), consistent with the figure cited in the main paper.

### Impossibility Metrics

Five metrics are tracked separately as impossibility diagnostics:

| Metric | VMS | Tier 1 | Tier 2 | Tier 3 | Tier 4 | Tier 5 | Tier 6 |
|--------|-----|--------|--------|--------|--------|--------|--------|
| Word-length AC(1) | +0.160 | +0.000 | +0.003 | +0.004 | −0.000 | +0.000 | +0.006 |
| Repeated-word rate | 0.0083 | 0.0077 | 0.0064 | 0.0031 | 0.0031 | 0.0045 | 0.0069 |
| MATTR-25 | 0.919 | 0.919 | 0.940 | 0.967 | 0.965 | 0.952 | 0.932 |
| SLSW rate | 4.47% | 6.30% | 5.31% | 2.01% | 1.96% | 2.83% | 4.27% |
| EV ratio (500/25) | 1.155 | 0.426 | 0.435 | 0.619 | 0.503 | 0.753 | 0.584 |

No tier simultaneously satisfies all three impossibility metrics (AC, repetition, MATTR-25). The Combined tier (Tier 6) achieves the highest AC (+0.006) and closest SLSW rate (4.27% vs VMS 4.47%) through folio-restricted copying, but at the cost of depressed lexical diversity — the impossibility triple described in §4.4.

## S3.5 Factorial Decomposition

A 2×2+1 factorial design confirms that the PGCS generator's components are independently necessary:

| Cell | Grammar | Lexicon | h₂ | Zipf R² | AC | Gallows |
|------|---------|---------|-----|---------|-----|---------|
| Full VMS | VMS | VMS | 2.10 | 0.88 | +0.105 | 21.1% |

| Lexicon only | Random | VMS | 3.35 | 0.88 | +0.01 | 7.5% |
| Neither | Random | Random | 3.35 | 0.45 | −0.01 | 7.5% |
| Morph-random | VMS | VMS (shuffled) | 2.12 | 0.82 | +0.08 | 20.5% |

Only the full model (VMS grammar + VMS lexicon) passes all metrics. Procedurally generated vocabularies fail on Zipf and hapax distributions. The VMS lexicon is learned or accumulated, not generated de novo from rules.


## S3.6 Position-Frequency Gradient

The position-frequency gradient (PFG) remains the only metric that no generator reproduces:

| System | PFG | Interpretation |
|--------|-----|----------------|
| VMS | −41.2 | Strong: rare words cluster at line edges |
| All generators | −1 to −2 | Flat: uniform frequency across positions |
| Natural language | −3 to −8 | Mild: some positional frequency variation |

The VMS PFG is 20× steeper than any tested system, implying a non-stationary production mechanism where the scribe's word-selection distribution shifts during line production.
