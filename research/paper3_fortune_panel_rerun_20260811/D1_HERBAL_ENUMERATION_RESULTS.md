# D1 strict-window herbal enumeration — Vienna–Klosterneuburg

Protocol frozen before search at commit `00e91a303fec017d74b80c29e5f45604f8a6c5d0`.
Date: 2026-08-11.

## Formal result

**NO QUALIFYING VIENNA–KLOSTERNEUBURG D1 WITNESS FOUND.**

This is a bounded negative result, not proof of historical absence. The search found abundant strict-window medical/herbal textual material in the Vienna–Klosterneuburg scholarly environment, but no manuscript satisfying all three conditions simultaneously: (i) medicinal-plant/herbal function; (ii) actual plant illustrations materially integrated with that content; and (iii) secure production or documented access in the Vienna–Klosterneuburg comparison system by 1438.

## Strongest strict-window local controls that fail only the illustration criterion

- Klosterneuburg Cod. 681: *Arzneibuch*, securely Klosterneuburg, 1396. Fully digitised. No qualifying plant-image cycle identified in catalogue/digital inspection.
- Klosterneuburg Cod. 684: composite astronomical/medical manuscript, second decade fifteenth century, with Vienna/Klosterneuburg localisation for constituent material. Includes Abū al-Qāsim, four alphabetical tables of medicinal herbs, recipes, and astronomy. Catalogue identifies astronomical drawings, not an illustrated herbal cycle.
- Klosterneuburg Cod. 680: medical Sammelhandschrift, c.1418–1420, formerly at St Andrä an der Traisen. Catalogue documents medical content and medicinal-plant/herb-book culture in its institutional context; no qualifying plant-image cycle is catalogued.
- Klosterneuburg Cod. 140: Arnaldus de Villanova / Petrus de Crescentiis, second half fourteenth century, tentatively Klosterneuburg. No qualifying plant-image cycle has been identified in the accessible catalogue evidence; retained as unresolved non-positive rather than treated as proof of absence.

## False positives rejected by the preregistered provenance rule

- ÖNB Cod. Ser. n. 2644, *Tacuinum sanitatis*, 1380–1399: 206 full-page miniatures and unquestionably relevant plant/medical imagery. However, ÖNB provenance identifies Georg von Liechtenstein, Bishop of Trento, followed later by Innsbruck/Ambras holdings; the manuscript was transferred from Ambras to Vienna only in 1806. Modern Vienna shelfmark therefore does not demonstrate Vienna access by 1438.
- Vienna Dioscorides: famous illustrated plant manuscript, but produced in Constantinople in the sixth century and documented in Vienna only in the early modern period; excluded.
- ÖNB Cod. 1118, 'Wiener Kräuterbuch': early textual herbal tradition, but associated with Mondsee and no qualifying plant-image cycle or strict-window Vienna access demonstrated.
- ÖNB Cod. 2864 / 2962 and comparable current-Vienna medical/herbal codices: current location does not establish medieval Vienna provenance; no qualifying strict-window Vienna plant-image cycle was identified.

## Near controls outside the strict cell

- Debrecen R 459, *Pflanzen- und Tierbuch*: genuinely illustrated Ps.-Apuleius herbal, dated second quarter fifteenth century, localised only to the Middle Bavarian linguistic region; medieval provenance is unknown. It proves illustrated herbal production in the broader Bavarian-Austrian cultural world near the window, but not Vienna–Klosterneuburg access by 1438.
- Petronell *Circa instans*: illustrated Austrian herbal, but dated middle/third quarter fifteenth century, outside the strict window.
- St Florian Cod. XI 641 and Salzburg M I 129: substantial herbal compilations, second half fifteenth century; outside strict window.

## Specialist-corpus cross-check

KdiH's herbal survey records three separate fifteenth-century Austrian–Bavarian Ps.-Apuleius translations represented today at Debrecen, Salzburg and Vienna and states that only the Debrecen witness is illustrated. This does not exhaust Latin illustrated herbals, so it is corroborating rather than dispositive; combined with the Manuscripta/ÖNB/Klosterneuburg title-family search it supports the bounded null.

## Italian positive controls under identical D1 definition

- Padua/Veneto: Carrara Herbal / *Liber agregà*, produced at Padua c.1390–1404, a medicinal herbal with extensive plant illustration. D1 = D0.
- Pavia/Milan/Lombardy: Casanatense MS 459, *Historia Plantarum*, produced in the Visconti court environment c.1395, with hundreds of natural-medical illustrations including plants. D1 = D0.

A separate survey of accurate informative medieval *Cucumis* illustrations (Paris, Janick & Daunay 2011) likewise found its 1300–1458 corpus in Italy and France, not Austria; this is a proxy for naturalistic illustration, not a complete census of all herbals.

## Interpretation

Vienna–Klosterneuburg remains **U** for D1. The evidential status is stronger than before: the asymmetry is no longer merely asymmetrical search effort. We have strict-window local evidence for herbal/medical knowledge but no qualifying local illustrated medicinal-herbal witness, while Padua and Pavia/Milan each have direct positive illustrated controls.

This does **not** make the broader Northern-Italian provenance conclusion robust. The Draft 15 sensitivity result remains unchanged: removing D1 still produces a four-way tie on D3–D6 among Padua–Venice, Pavia–Milan, Bologna and Vienna–Klosterneuburg. What the enumeration changes is the credibility of retaining D1 as an observed manuscript-ecology difference, not the fact that the overall geographic ranking depends materially on D1.

## Highest-value remaining falsifier

Any manuscript shown to meet all three conditions — medicinal herbal, actual plant illustrations, and secure Vienna–Klosterneuburg production/access by 1438 — immediately converts Vienna D1 from U to covered and erases the present D1 distinction. The most useful future archival target is therefore not another current-Vienna herbal but a pre-1439 Vienna/Klosterneuburg library or ownership record identifying an illustrated herbal exemplar.
