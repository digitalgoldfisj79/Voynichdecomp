# Vaduz–Montfort–Fugger–Geizkofler–Widemann late-provenance audit

Date: 2026-09-04
Programme: Rhine–Bodensee–Upper Rhine Corridor v0.2
Status: **VERY STRONG DOCUMENTED NETWORK / CHAIN OF CUSTODY UNRESOLVED**

## Question

Recover and test the previously noted possible connection between the intermediary in Karl Widemann's 1599 sale of books to Rudolf II and the Montfort/Vaduz/Burgundy network. This is a late-provenance test, not evidence for fifteenth-century manufacture.

## Result in one sentence

The remembered link was real and denser than expected: **Zacharias Geizkofler** administered the Widemann→Rudolf book transfer and independently produced/compiled a 1574–76 legal deduction on the Montfort-Tettnang succession; his **uncle Lukas Geizkofler**, with whom Zacharias travelled toward Italy in 1574, personally encountered Ulrich von Montfort in Besançon and later served as a long-term Fugger syndic in Montfort/Fugger inheritance litigation. The inheriting Montfort branch was itself directly Fugger-descended. The direct missing edge remains an identifiable Montfort book/manuscript entering Widemann's collection or the 1599 Rudolf consignment.

## 1. Genealogical spine from the 1437 Vaduz/Schellenberg line

Authority synthesis in Neue Deutsche Biographie supports the descent relevant to the collection line:

- Wilhelm V von Montfort-Tettnang (d.1439), party to the 1437 Schellenberg/Eschnerberg settlement;
- son Hugo XIII (d.1491), inheriting Rothenfels/Argen/Wasserburg;
- son Hugo XV (d.1519);
- son Hugo XVI (d.1564), humanistically educated diplomat;
- son Ulrich, commonly numbered Ulrich IX in regional scholarship (NDB uses Ulrich IV), d.1574, last older Tettnang/Rothenfels-line count and collector.

This is a genealogical continuity statement only. It does not imply continuous custody of any specific book from 1437 to 1574.

## 2. Ulrich's collection is specifically relevant to learned/occult book culture

The 1574 inventory and scholarship on Ulrich's Kunstkammer describe a collection including:

- Kunstbücher;
- geomantic works;
- books of music;
- astronomical instruments;
- antiquities, coins, pictures and curiosities;
- boxes containing alchemical/testing material (`alchimisterei und probiersachen`).

Michael Toxites dedicated a Paracelsian medical edition to Ulrich in 1567, explicitly praising his study of medical art and referring to benefits received from him. This establishes an actual Paracelsian/medical intellectual connection, not merely generic collecting taste.

A surviving object supplies an independent procurement/provenance anchor: the KHM dragon-shaped drinking horn bears **Ulrich IX Montfort-Tettnang's arms**, was mounted by Augsburg goldsmith Cornelius Groß c.1560/70, and later passed from Ulrich's estate into Ferdinand II's collection. Thus Ulrich's collection had a demonstrable **Tettnang↔Augsburg** procurement link. This is not evidence for book procurement, but it makes Augsburg a real collection-market node rather than a retrospective coincidence.

## 3. Zacharias Geizkofler: direct Montfort legal-document connection

Alexander Sigelen (2009), citing Staatsarchiv Ludwigsburg B 90, old Bü 576, records a deduction by Zacharias Geizkofler concerning the succession of the sons of Count Jakob von Montfort-Bregenz to Tettnang-Langenargen after the death of Count Ulrich von Montfort-Tettnang, dated approximately 1574–76.

Zacharias was born in 1560. Therefore this must be treated as evidence of legal education/access to Montfort succession material, **not** as proof that the teenage Zacharias officially brokered or counselled the succession.

A separate Montfort-house manuscript begun in 1575, `Allerley Schriften, Documenta das Haus Montfort betreffend`, probably by Dr Math. Raymann, contains a legal deduction arguing the inheritance rights of Georg von Montfort and his younger brothers and copies supporting charters in extenso. The relationship between this Montfort-house dossier and Geizkofler's Bü 576 deduction is an explicit open test; no derivation is currently claimed.

The Geizkofler archive was fully digitised in 2025–26, but the old Sammelfaszikel numbering was dissolved during retrospective cataloguing. The present digital identifier corresponding to old Bü 576 has not yet been resolved from the public index. Sigelen's exact archival citation remains the authority for this edge.

## 4. Lukas Geizkofler: the Burgundy memory — and his direct link to Zacharias

Lukas Geizkofler's autobiography, edited by Adam Wolf, records the episode `Graf Montfort und Dr Bemler in Besançon`: Count Ulrich von Montfort invited Lukas to dinner in Besançon during Lukas's French/Burgundian study travels. Lukas subsequently studied at Dôle and obtained his doctorate there.

Ulrich himself had studied at Dôle in 1544. Thus the remembered “Burgundy” component is real and person-specific, not a generic regional overlap.

More importantly, Lukas was **Zacharias Geizkofler's uncle**. Modern work on Lukas's autobiography records that in autumn 1574 Lukas travelled from Augsburg toward Italy accompanied by his nephew Zacharias. Zacharias is independently documented at Padua in this educational period. The timing overlaps the 1574–76 Montfort legal deduction.

This is a strong family-network fact, but the following inference remains forbidden: we do **not** know that Lukas supplied Zacharias with the Montfort succession case or papers.

Later scholarship shows Lukas, as Fugger lawyer/syndic in Augsburg, played a substantial long-term role in legal disputes joining the Montfort and Fugger families and achieved a settlement in 1596.

## 5. The Montfort succession was structurally a Fugger network

This is the largest upgrade from the hostile re-check.

Jakob von Montfort-Bregenz/Pfannberg married **Katharina Fugger**, daughter of Anton Fugger, in 1553. Their sons were the claimants/successors to the Swabian Tettnang/Argen inheritance after Ulrich's death. A historical Montfort account states that five sons — Johann VI, Georg III, Sigmund, Anton I and Wolfgang — claimed the Swabian lordships and were placed in possession in 1576 subject to conditions.

Thus the very Montfort successors in Zacharias's 1574–76 deduction were **Fugger descendants**.

A Bavarian Reichskammergericht inventory strengthens this further. In litigation beginning 1580 it states that the division of Count Ulrich's estate remained unresolved between one set of heirs and **Count Georg III von Montfort, Marx Fugger and Hans Fugger** on the other side; the case file includes a **Stuttgarter Kompromiß of 1580** concerning the inheritance of the extinct Montfort-Rothenfels line.

The exact procedural/legal capacity of Marx and Hans Fugger must remain case-specific; they are not automatically labelled owners of Ulrich's movable collection. But they were demonstrably inside the estate litigation.

This matches Völkel's independent characterization of Lukas Geizkofler as a long-term Fugger syndic in Montfort/Fugger legal disputes.

Therefore the sequence is no longer simply:

`Montfort estate — (possible Fugger interest) — Augsburg`.

It is:

`Montfort succession → Fugger-descended claimants + named Fugger estate parties → Fugger legal network → Lukas Geizkofler → Augsburg`, while Lukas is Zacharias's uncle.

That is a documented social/legal transmission network. It is still not a manuscript-custody chain.

## 6. Ulrich's Kunstkammer dispersal

After Ulrich's death, competition for his collection involved Munich, Innsbruck and the Fuggers. Heirs' disputes delayed transfer. Scholarship states that a substantial part of the collection was not transferred to Archduke Ferdinand II's Ambras collection until c.1590 and explicitly describes the Montfort collection as including books among antiquities, coins, works of art, medals and similar objects.

Object-level transfer is independently demonstrated by surviving Montfort objects in the Ambras/KHM collections, notably the Augsburg-made dragon drinking horn with Ulrich's arms.

### Critical negative

No delivery list for the Montfort→Ambras transfer survives in the evidence reviewed. Therefore:

- not all books can be assumed to have gone to Ambras;
- no individual `Kunstbuch` has yet been traced across the transfer;
- books could in principle have been separated during the prolonged inheritance dispute, but that is a hypothesis, not a finding.

## 7. Who brokered the Montfort→Ambras acquisition?

Modern Quiccheberg commentary identifies **Wilhelm Werner von Zimmern**, a confidant of Ferdinand II, as instrumental in Ferdinand's acquisition of Ulrich's Kunstkammer.

Zacharias Geizkofler entered Ferdinand II's service in 1585 and remained associated with him through 1595, so he was in the correct institutional environment when the collection finally moved around 1590. However, **no evidence reviewed identifies Zacharias as broker of the Montfort acquisition**.

This negative is permanently retained.

## 8. Zacharias Geizkofler: Widemann→Rudolf transfer, 1599

The actual later book-transfer edge is primary-documentary.

Austrian Hoffinanz records dated 1 March 1599 order Reichspfennigmeister Zacharias Geizkofler to pay Augsburg physician Karl Widemann 500 Taler for books, receive them and arrange their transport. Geizkofler family accounts preserve the corresponding payment as 600 Rhenish gulden. A 1600 transport entry describes the shipment as a small barrel containing `allerlai selzamen büchern` and records its forwarding to the late Hans Popp, Rudolf II's chamberlain.

Thus:

**Widemann → Geizkofler → Rudolf II** is a verified book-transaction mechanism.

It remains a candidate for the Voynich transaction, not proof that the Voynich was in the batch.

## 10. Additional Rudolfine-court bridge: Trautson

Neue Deutsche Biographie provides a further concrete social intersection:

- Paul Sixt Trautson's second wife (1591) was Anna Popel von Lobkowitz, widow of Georg IV Count von Montfort-Bregenz-Peckach-Tettnang (d.1590), a member of the line inheriting Tettnang after Ulrich;
- Trautson protected/patronized the Lutheran Reichspfennigmeister Zacharias Geizkofler at Rudolf's court;
- a Latin poem from Zacharias to Trautson survives in the Ferdinandeum collection.

This creates another Montfort-household↔Geizkofler court-network bridge immediately around the Widemann transaction period. It is not a book-custody edge.
