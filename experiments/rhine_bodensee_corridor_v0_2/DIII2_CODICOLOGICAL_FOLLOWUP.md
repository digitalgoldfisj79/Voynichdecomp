# Basel UBH D III 2 — codicological follow-up

Date: 2026-09-04
Programme: Rhine–Bodensee–Upper Rhine Corridor v0.2
Status: **COMPOSITE-LEVEL POSITIVE; PRODUCTION-UNIT RELATION UNRESOLVED**

## Question

Does UBH D III 2 actually demonstrate a single c.1420 copying/production unit in which the Salernitan `Circa instans` was integrated with Latin–German pharmaceutical/herbal vocabulary in the Heidelberg–Basel network associated with Johannes Burkardi?

## Verified structure

Repository structural metadata gives:

- ff. 198r–226v: `Circa instans`, Matthaeus Platearius
- ff. 227ra–229vb: `Synonyma Antidotario Nicolai Salernitani applicata`, Latin–German
- ff. 230ra–234r: `Vocabularium herbarum Latine - Germanice`
- ff. 234ra–235vb: `Quid pro quo`
- ff. 236ra–263v: Guido de Cauliaco, `Chirurgia magna`

The Handschriftencensus codicological record gives one-column layout generally but **two columns on ff.227–262**. Therefore f.227 is a real layout discontinuity: the `Circa instans` block ends at f.226v, and the bilingual pharmaceutical apparatus begins with a change from one to two columns. The two-column format then continues into Guido's `Chirurgia`.

## Dating caution

The codex is catalogued `um 1420`, but the explicit date used by Handschriftencensus is on **f.140r**, not inside ff.198–235. The whole-codex date must therefore not be silently converted into a folio-specific date for the herbal/pharmaceutical blocks.

## Burkardi caution

Schmidt (1919), Scarpatetti (1977) and Handschriftencensus state that the codex is **partly** in the hand of Johannes Burkardi / associated with him. Steinmann independently documents Burkardi copying at Heidelberg on 31 January 1423 and being in Basel by 1437. However, no online source recovered in this pass assigns either ff.198–226 or ff.227–235 specifically to Burkardi's hand.

The surviving claim is therefore a **book-network association**, not a folio-level autograph attribution.

## Functional relation

The sequence `Synonyma Nicolai` → plant vocabulary → `Quid pro quo` is coherent pharmaceutical reference material. Ventura's work on the `Circa instans` tradition notes that abbreviated simples lists, synonymaries, weights/measures and related materials occur with the `Antidotarium Nicolai` as practical access/use apparatus. Thus the f.227–235 block is functionally coherent rather than a random vernacular insertion.

That does not prove that the f.198–226 `Circa instans` and f.227–235 apparatus were copied in one campaign.

## What is established

1. An early-fifteenth-century medical composite preserves `Circa instans` adjacent to Latin–German Salernitan/pharmaceutical and plant-nomenclature material.
2. The codex is associated in part with Johannes Burkardi, whose Heidelberg (1423) → Basel (by 1437) book/scribal trajectory is documented.
3. The bilingual pharmaceutical block begins at a deliberate reference-layout change and is internally coherent.

## What is NOT established

1. ff.198–235 are one codicological unit.
2. ff.198–235 share one paper stock or watermark sequence.
3. ff.198–235 are all c.1420 specifically.
4. ff.198–235 were copied in Basel or Heidelberg.
5. Burkardi copied the `Circa instans`, `Synonyma` or `Vocabularium` folios specifically.
6. The Latin–German vocabulary has been securely localised dialectally to Alemannic/Upper Rhine.
7. The unit contains botanical illustration.

## Evidential consequence

Previous wording — `c.1420 D III 2 combines Salernitan Circa instans with Latin-German herbal nomenclature` — is too compressed because it implies a single contemporary production unit.

Revised wording:

> **UBH D III 2 is an early-fifteenth-century medical composite in the Burkardi Heidelberg–Basel book network that preserves `Circa instans` immediately before a coherent Latin–German pharmaceutical/herbal reference apparatus. The source traditions are demonstrably co-present in the historical composite, but a single c.1420 Heidelberg/Basel copying unit has not been established.**

Supabase `herbal_transmission` has therefore been revised to `qualified_composite_level_positive_unit_level_unresolved` with evidence level `moderate`. The primary-window flag remains qualified-positive at composite level; Paper 3 D1 illustrated-medicinal-plant access remains unresolved/negative for scoring.

## Hard documentary boundary

The public online descriptions do not expose a full quire formula, watermark analysis, or folio-specific hand map for D III 2. Handschriftencensus points to **Günther Goldschmidt, unpublished description (1940), 38 leaves, Basel UB Special Reading Room**, and Scarpatetti's dated-manuscript catalogue. Those are the highest-value remaining sources for resolving the unit-level question. No broader web search should substitute for them.
