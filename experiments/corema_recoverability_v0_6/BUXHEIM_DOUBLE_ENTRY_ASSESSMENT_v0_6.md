# Buxheim Organ Book double-entry assessment — v0.6

**Date:** 2026-07-25  
**Decision:** do not launch manual double transcription as part of the current Voynich transfer programme.

## Source and legal position

The Bayerische Staatsbibliothek identifies the Buxheim Organ Book as Mus.ms. 3725, created around 1460–1470, with 174 leaves. The digital object is marked Public Domain and its metadata CC0. The source uses older German organ tablature: the upper voice is written on a staff and lower voices in letter notation.

Official facsimile and metadata:

- https://www.bavarikon.de/object/bav:BSB-MUS-00000BSB00104633?lang=en

A public-domain 1887 Robert Eitner transcription supplies 50 excerpts in common notation. A later complete Wallner edition is not public domain in the EU. Catalogue page:

- https://imslp.org/wiki/Buxheimer_Orgelbuch_(Various)

## Scientific value

Buxheim could support a historically appropriate **notation-to-event** benchmark. A facsimile/transcription alignment would test whether surface signs and layout recover musical events under held-out piece or hand conditions. It would be useful for the general distinction between notation recognition and operational recovery.

It is not, however, an appropriate replacement for CoReMA’s **procedural semantic-role** calibration. Buxheim contains musical notation, not a corpus of independently annotated action/ingredient/tool/output roles. A positive Buxheim result would therefore not repair either failed CoReMA gate and could not authorise a recipe-like or procedural interpretation of Voynich.

## Cost-benefit decision after CoReMA

The completed CoReMA result already shows:

- strong real procedural sequence order;
- near-threshold lexical role recovery;
- failed identity-neutral role recovery under manuscript holdout;
- no admissible Voynich transfer.

Manual Buxheim transcription would be labour-intensive and would answer a neighbouring question. It would risk becoming a diversion from the failed semantic-transfer calibration. It is therefore rejected as a required v0.6 continuation.

## Optional future benchmark

A separate historical-notation methods study could preregister a small legally clean benchmark using the BSB facsimile and Eitner’s public-domain excerpts:

- 12 pieces sampled before transcription, stratified by manuscript region and notation complexity;
- two independently entered excerpts per piece;
- independent double entry by two transcribers;
- blind adjudication of disagreements;
- explicit facsimile coordinates and source identifiers;
- alignment error, event recovery and identity-neutral transfer reported by held-out piece;
- no Voynich scoring and no semantic extrapolation.

This optional benchmark should proceed only if E-LAUTE cannot supply aligned MEI at adequate scale or if a paper specifically requires independent organ-tablature validation.

## Closure

The acquisition-plan question is resolved: Buxheim is scientifically relevant but not decision-relevant to the present CoReMA failure, so manual double entry is not initiated.
