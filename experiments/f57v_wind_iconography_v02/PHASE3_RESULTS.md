## Endpoint results after round 1

### P3-E1 — pre-1500 opposed-pair facing distinction
**NOT MET.** No independently verified pre-1500 comparator was found with four axial anthropomorphs where one opposed pair systematically faces outward and the perpendicular pair inward.

### P3-E2 — 1300–1450 witness
**NOT MET.** Period-near wind/cosmographic comparators were found, but none currently verifies the f57v opposed-pair facing polarity.

### P3-E3 — northern-Italian / Alpine / Germanic witness
**NOT MET.** The relevant regions preserve wind diagrams and anthropomorphic wind iconography, but no exact or strong-partial opposed-facing analogue has been verified.

### P3-E4 — wind specificity
**UNRESOLVED.** The exact facing signature has not yet been found in either wind or non-wind controls. Importantly, standard wind examples currently point the other way: Walters W.73 is uniformly centre-directed, while early principal-wind figures are described as a common axial class rather than two opposed orientation classes.

### P3-E5 — alternating 2-1-2-1 gesture pattern
**NOT MET.** No historical comparator with a verified equivalent alternating gesture-count pattern has been found. The strongest non-wind morphological analogue, Troyes MS 252 f.14, is explicitly 2-2-2-2.

## Main result
The corrected f57v signature is **more unusual than the earlier fourfold-wind hypothesis predicted**.

This weakens a straightforward reading of f57v as a conventional medieval wind rota. Canonical wind diagrams can strongly support fourfold/cardinal anthropomorphism, concentric organization, and personified winds, but the currently screened examples do not reproduce the target's opposed-axis posture code.

At the same time, the result does not erase the broader historical relationship established in phases 1–2. Medieval cosmological diagrams repeatedly organize information as orthogonal oppositions, and the wind / Paradise-river / element / season / humour traditions exchange graphical structures. The evidence therefore now favours one of two narrower models over a literal conventional-wind reading:

1. **hybrid / transformed descendant** — a wind/cosmographic grammar was retained but its four figures were reassigned differentiated functions;
2. **non-wind quaternity** — f57v uses the broader fourfold cosmological diagram language, with winds only one historical cousin rather than the intended subject.

## Strong new discriminator
The key feature is no longer `four people around a circle`. It is the **D2-like axis polarity**:
- one opposed pair has outward facial orientation and two transverse gestures;
- the perpendicular pair has inward facial orientation and one secure gesture each.

The next programme should test whether this polarity maps onto a medieval *pair-of-opposites* system (e.g. hot/cold vs wet/dry, vertical/light-heavy vs horizontal qualities, world parts, seasons, elemental transformations) and whether the f57v gestures index specific annular text positions. If gesture endpoints align reproducibly with token/ring boundaries, the figures may function as diagrammatic pointers or operators rather than merely as personifications.

## Caution
Round 1 is a targeted historical screen, not an exhaustive catalogue of every medieval quaternity diagram. `NOT MET` means no qualified match was found in the screened evidence; it does not prove that no such witness exists.
