# PREREGISTRATION — f57v Wind Iconography v0.2
Date frozen: 2026-08-09

## Motivation
v0.1 returned `NO TEST` because the automated visual coder failed its positive-control qualification. v0.2 does not repair or tune that instrument. It tests a different historical proposition using independently catalogued wind iconography.

## Primary question
Does medieval wind iconography independently attest the compound grammar seen as a live possibility on f57v — four principal anthropomorphs in an axial/circular arrangement, centre-related arm/hand gestures, and a hierarchical four-principal/eight-collateral system — and how close in date and geography can that grammar be traced to 1400–1450?

## Source policy
Primary source spine: Thomas Raff, `Die Ikonografie der mittelalterlichen Windpersonifikation`, Aachener Kunstblätter 48 (1978–79), 71–218.
Supplement: Barbara Obrist, `Wind Diagrams and Medieval Cosmology`, Speculum 72/1 (1997), 33–84, especially diagrammatic traditions not exhausted by Raff.

Every witness counted as `verified` must have a repository/shelfmark/folio or stable object identifier, a defensible date range, and independent identification as wind imagery from an authoritative catalogue or scholarly source. Voynich-adjacent literature is excluded from witness selection and labels.

## Frozen morphology fields
A. `circular_concentric`: circular or concentric cosmological/rotational framework.
B. `four_principals`: four principal human/anthropomorphic winds represented as the major agents.
C. `axial_cardinal`: the four principals occupy vertical/horizontal axes, cardinal positions, or equivalent four-world-direction positions.
D. `extended_hand_arm_relation`: hands/arms materially relate principals to centre, subordinate agents, or direction structure.
E. `held_subordinate_heads`: principal figures hold/control subordinate wind heads or busts.
F. `four_plus_eight`: explicit or unambiguous four-principal + eight-collateral = twelve hierarchy.
G. `breath_horn`: explicit breath stream, blowing mouth, trumpet/horn, or equivalent wind emission.
H. `central_body`: discrete central world/body/emblem/personification.

Each field is `present`, `absent`, or `unclear`. Ambiguity may not be upgraded by reading Voynich text.

## Endpoints
### E1 — Historical existence
PASS if >=1 independently attested medieval wind witness has A+B+C+D and at least one of E/F.

### E2 — Exact hierarchy
PASS if >=1 independently attested witness has B+E+F.

### E3 — Chronological bridge
For each of the two compounds below, record the latest securely dated pre-1450 witness:
- compound X = A+B+C+D;
- compound Y = B+E+F.
No arbitrary pass/fail year is imposed. The temporal gap to the Voynich radiocarbon interval is reported directly.

### E4 — Late-medieval component continuity
Identify securely dated witnesses >=1350 and separately >=1400 retaining at least two of A–G. This tests persistence of the vocabulary, not persistence of the exact compound.

### E5 — Geographic sensitivity
Enumerate verified witnesses from northern Italy, Alpine regions, and southern/central German lands. Provenance ambiguity is reported, not resolved by convenience.

## Population accounting
Raff is read end-to-end and converted into an auditable witness table where feasible. Search-hit counts are not prevalence estimates. No p-value or frequency denominator will be claimed until distinct witnesses have been deduplicated and denominator inclusion is explicit.

## f57v target coding
The target is described independently from the historical witnesses:
- A present;
- B present;
- C present;
- D present at ensemble level (four rotationally distributed arm/hand forms entering the central field);
- E unclear/absent as an observed fact: no subordinate heads are clearly held;
- F unknown: no semantics inferred from Voynich writing;
- G absent/unclear: no unambiguous breath streams;
- H present: six-lobed central object with central dot.

This coding is frozen before final v0.2 chronological enumeration. The six-lobed object is not assumed to be a cloud.

## Interpretation
- Passing E1/E2 establishes historical precedent, not identification of f57v.
- A short chronological/geographic bridge would materially strengthen a wind-family interpretation.
- A long chronological gap weakens direct transmission claims but does not erase precedent.
- No result can establish a specific exemplar without stemmatic or uniquely diagnostic evidence.
