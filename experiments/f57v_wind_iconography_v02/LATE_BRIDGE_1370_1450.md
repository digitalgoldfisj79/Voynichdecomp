# LATE BRIDGE 1370–1450 — f57v Wind Iconography v0.2
Date: 2026-08-09

This note isolates the period immediately before and overlapping the Voynich radiocarbon window. It distinguishes exact 4+8 rota continuity from persistence of component iconographic tokens.

## 1. British Library Yates Thompson MS 10, f.11v — c.1370–1390, Paris

The British Library catalogue explicitly describes f.11v as:
- four angels at the four corners of the earth;
- the winds represented by four animal heads;
- the angels **hold the winds**.

This is not a 4+8 cosmological rota and therefore cannot qualify as an exact `four principals + eight collateral heads` positive. It is nevertheless decisive for a narrower chronological issue: the convention of a wind being represented as a detached head that is physically handled/controlled by an anthropomorphic agent was still active in elite manuscript illumination in the later fourteenth century.

Classification: `late hand-held wind-head token persistence — PASS`.

## 2. Giusto de' Menabuoi, Padua Baptistery Apocalypse — 1375–1378

The Museo Diocesano di Padova dates Giusto's complete Baptistery fresco programme to 1375–1378 and states that it includes an Apocalypse cycle. A surviving image is independently catalogued under the subject `Four winds, Battistero di Padova`.

A dedicated pixel-only audit was run on that `Four winds` image with Qwen2.5-VL-3B-Instruct after the historical label had been fixed from external sources. The model described:
- four winds as disembodied heads;
- angels interacting with the heads by holding/blocking them with their hands;
- a non-rota narrative spatial organization.

Because the exact hand/head morphology is presently machine-read rather than stated in the museum catalogue, the visual subclaim is coded `strong auxiliary / requires human visual confirmation`, not catalogue-grade fact.

Classification: `northern-Italian four-wind + hand-control bridge — PASS for scene/date/geography; morphology machine-audited`.

This is geographically important: it places a four-winds + hand-control representational environment in **Padua** roughly 30–60 years before the Voynich parchment window.

## 3. Konrad Kyeser, Bellifortis, Besançon BM MS 1360, f.4v — first half 15th c., Germany

Biblissima/Initiale dates f.4v to 1400–1449, gives Germany as place of origin, identifies the text as Kyeser's `Bellifortis`, and labels the image `Vents, Soleil et Lune`.

This is not a rota and does not preserve held subordinate heads. It does show that full anthropomorphic wind imagery integrated with celestial/atmospheric imagery remains current **inside the Voynich radiocarbon window**.

Classification: `contemporary anthropomorphic-wind component continuity — PASS`.

## 4. Galatina, Santa Caterina d'Alessandria — first half 15th c., Apulia

Raff associates the Galatina Apocalypse frescoes with the late-medieval `holding/restraining the winds` tradition and describes the wind heads there as grotesque human heads. Independent modern cataloguing verifies the major Apocalypse fresco cycle and its first-half-15th-century date/location, but the precise four-winds panel has not yet been independently located and pixel-audited in this programme.

Classification: `Italian contemporary auxiliary bridge — QUARANTINED pending exact-panel verification`.

## 5. Revised chronological conclusion

The exact early compound — four complete principal winds each encoding two subordinate winds as held heads — remains unverified in a securely dated 1400–1450 northern-Italian/Alpine rota.

However, two formerly separate chronological objections are now substantially answered:

1. **Did the wind-head / human-hand interaction survive late enough?** Yes. Yates Thompson MS 10 supplies explicit catalogue-grade evidence c.1370–1390; Padua 1375–78 supplies a geographically relevant scene with machine-confirmed matching morphology.
2. **Did anthropomorphic four-wind imagery remain current in the Voynich period?** Yes. Kyeser's first-half-15th-century `Bellifortis` witness is directly inside the window, and the Italian material continues around it.

Therefore an objection framed as `the relevant wind iconography was already obsolete by 1400` is untenable. The remaining issue is not survival but **specificity**: whether f57v is better explained by this transmitted wind grammar than by non-wind fourfold rota traditions.

## 6. Next falsification target

The next useful search should not collect more generic wind heads. It should attempt to falsify the hypothesis by building a matched population of 1300–1450 non-wind circular diagrams with:
- exactly four axial anthropomorphs;
- repeated arm/hand gestures toward a common centre;
- concentric annuli;
- central emblem/body;
- possible 4/8/12 modular structure.

If that conjunction is common outside wind/cosmography, the f57v resemblance loses specificity. If it remains concentrated in wind-related material, the hypothesis strengthens.
