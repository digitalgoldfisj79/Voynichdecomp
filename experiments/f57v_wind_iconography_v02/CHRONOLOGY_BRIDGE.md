## Chronological inference

The data no longer support a simple `early-medieval motif -> 200-year gap -> Voynich` description. There is a documented sequence:

1. exact 4-principal / 8-collateral held-head morphology in the 11th–early 13th centuries;
2. an Italian `Liber Nemroth` witness continuing the `Four cosmic winds` tradition at the end of the 13th century, with exact morphology still to be checked;
3. ca.1300–1330 Austrian/Bavarian descendants preserving the fourfold/twelve-wind grammar while transforming, reducing, reinterpreting, or abstracting the figures;
4. a contemporaneous Florentine wind-cosmography programme in `La sfera` ca.1420–1430;
5. abundant later-15th-century Italian anthropomorphic wind-diagram continuity in Florence, Padua and probably Verona.

The still-open historical question is narrow: **does a securely dated ca.1300–1450 northern-Italian/Alpine witness preserve the exact principal-figure + two subordinate-heads-per-principal device, or an unmistakably close derivative?**

## Implication for f57v

Current evidence favors testing f57v as a possible **transformed descendant of a wind-cosmographic diagram grammar**, not as a literal copy of a single early medieval exemplar. This model predicts that semantic hierarchy can survive while iconographic tokens change, exactly as demonstrated in the Petrus Pictaviensis transmission.
