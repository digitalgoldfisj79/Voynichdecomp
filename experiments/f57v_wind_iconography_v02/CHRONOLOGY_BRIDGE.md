## Chronological inference

The data no longer support a simple `early-medieval motif -> 200-year gap -> Voynich` description. There is a documented sequence:

1. exact 4-principal / 8-collateral held-head morphology in the 11th–early 13th centuries;
2. an Italian `Liber Nemroth` witness continuing the `Four cosmic winds` tradition at the end of the 13th century, with exact morphology still to be checked;
3. ca.1300–1330 Austrian/Bavarian descendants preserving the fourfold/twelve-wind grammar while transforming, reducing, reinterpreting, or abstracting the figures;
4. by ca.1370–1390, the **wind-head-as-handled-object** token remains explicitly alive in French Apocalypse illumination (Yates Thompson MS 10), while Padua 1375–78 supplies geographically crucial northern-Italian four-wind / hand-control imagery;
5. in the Voynich radiocarbon window itself, Kyeser's `Bellifortis` preserves anthropomorphic four-wind imagery in Germany and Dati's `La sfera` provides a Florentine wind-cosmography programme;
6. later-15th-century Florence, Padua and probably Verona retain substantial anthropomorphic wind-diagram traditions.

This changes the historical model. The best-supported path is not necessarily continuous copying of one exact `four men each holding two heads` exemplar. Instead, **two component traditions demonstrably survive and can recombine**:

- the circular/cardinal/twelve-wind cosmographic architecture;
- the human-hand / wind-head interaction token.

Both remain active much later than the earliest exact compound.

The still-open historical question is narrow: **does a securely dated ca.1300–1450 northern-Italian/Alpine witness preserve the exact principal-figure + two subordinate-heads-per-principal device, or an unmistakably close rota derivative?**

## Implication for f57v

Current evidence favors testing f57v as a possible **transformed descendant of a wind-cosmographic diagram grammar**, not as a literal copy of a single early medieval exemplar. This model predicts that semantic hierarchy can survive while iconographic tokens change, exactly as demonstrated in the Petrus Pictaviensis transmission.

The Padua and Yates Thompson witnesses substantially weaken a narrower objection that `hands interacting with disembodied wind heads` was an obsolete early-medieval device by the late fourteenth century. They do **not** establish that f57v's small hand-adjacent forms are heads; that feature remains conservatively coded absent/unknown on the target.
