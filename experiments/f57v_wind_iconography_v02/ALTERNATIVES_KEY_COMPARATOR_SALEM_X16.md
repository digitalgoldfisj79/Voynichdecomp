# KEY COMPARATOR — Heidelberg Cod. Sal. X 16 f.2v

Date: 2026-08-09

## Why this comparator matters

Simona Cohen's study of medieval temporal/cosmological imagery describes the rota at Heidelberg, Cod. Sal. X 16 f.2v as a **multi-layer cosmological diagram in which several different tetradic systems coexist but are represented by different visual classes**:

- a concentric ring contains heads of the four cardinal directions (`oriens`, `auster`, `occidens`, `aquilo`);
- another zone contains weather/climate allegories;
- a further ring contains **four large personifications related to times of day and climatic conditions**, with zodiac signs between them;
- wind personifications occupy the corners **outside** the circular scheme.

The upper large figure is identified as Aurora/Dawn in Cohen's analysis.

## Consequence for f57v

This is a crucial control against semantic shortcutting. Even when a medieval rota contains explicit winds and directional information, the visually prominent set of **four large human figures inside the circular scheme need not be the winds**. Different rings can encode different but correlated quaternities: direction, climate, time-of-day, zodiac, winds.

Therefore, resemblance between f57v's four inner humans and wind-diagram architecture does not imply that those four humans themselves personify winds. A stronger alternative is that the f57v figures belong to a **temporal/climatic or other correlated quaternity layer inside a broader cosmographic diagram**.

This supports the ranked alternatives in `ALTERNATIVES_DEEP_RESEARCH.md`, especially:
1. tetradic correlation / quaternity grammar;
2. operator/indexer roles;
3. paired-opposites macrocosm/microcosm;
4. solar/time-of-day/life-cycle.

## Source

Simona Cohen, `Transformations of Time and Temporality in Medieval and Renaissance Art` (Brill), discussion of Heidelberg Cod. Sal. X 16 f.2v. The manuscript itself is Heidelberg University Library Cod. Sal. X 16, ca.1200/13th-century illumination tradition.

This comparator is early and therefore cannot by itself prove fifteenth-century survival of the exact arrangement. Its value is typological: it demonstrates that one rota can assign four separate visual systems to directions, weather, time/climate personifications, zodiac and winds rather than making the central/large anthropomorphs semantically identical with the winds.
