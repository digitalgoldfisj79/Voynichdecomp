## 1. Frozen target morphology

N/E/S/W are page positions only, not semantic cardinal directions.

- N: faces outward; two secure gestures approximately E + W.
- E: faces inward; one secure gesture approximately W.
- S: faces outward; two secure gestures approximately E + W.
- W: faces inward; one secure gesture approximately S.
- Secure gesture-count signature: `2-1-2-1`.
- Opposed-axis distinction: vertical pair N/S = outward + two gestures; horizontal pair E/W = inward + one secure gesture.
- Concentric/annular textual architecture present.
- Four axial anthropomorphs present.
- Centre remains an unidentified six-lobed/scalloped form with central dot.
- No secure breath streams; no secure held wind heads.

Phase 3 found no qualified exact or strong-partial pre-1500 analogue for the full opposed-facing + `2-1-2-1` signature. Phase 4 therefore asks what *classes of medieval diagram* can generate the observed architecture without assuming that the four humans are four equivalent wind personifications.

## 2. Main conclusion

The strongest alternative is **not** `elements`, `seasons`, `humours`, `ages`, or `winds` taken one at a time. Medieval scientific diagrams repeatedly correlate several such quaternities in a single wheel. The best historical model class is therefore:

> **an integrated tetradic concordance / cosmological-computational rota in which several fourfold systems are aligned across concentric or quartered structures, with the human figures potentially acting as personifications, operators, pointers, or state markers.**

This changes the question from `Which four things are the humans?` to `What relationships among several fourfold/cyclical systems are the humans and rings encoding?`

The wind hypothesis remains historically relevant because wind is frequently one layer in these concordances. It is no longer privileged as the complete semantic identity of f57v.

## 3. Highest-value historical evidence

### 3.1 Henry Daniel, `Liber uricrisiarum`: near-period medical quaternity

Cambridge University Library describes MS Ff.2.6 f.17r as a **diagram of quaternities** illustrating shared hot/cold and dry/moist characteristics of the **humours, regions, winds, seasons, elements and stages of life**. Henry Daniel composed and revised the `Liber uricrisiarum` c.1375–1382.

Wellcome MS 225 is an **early fifteenth-century** witness of the same text. The Wellcome catalogue explicitly records at f.16v a **circular pen-drawn figure of the Seasons, Winds, Humours, etc.**

Implication: a multi-domain circular quaternity diagram is not merely an early-medieval fossil. It survives into the early fifteenth century in a medical context close to the Voynich chronological window.

### 3.2 NYPL MA 069: one chart explicitly combines nearly all candidate semantics

The New York Public Library catalogues f.38v of a computus manuscript (1240–1260) as a **circular chart correlating directions, winds, elements, seasons, temperaments, qualities, and ages**.

Implication: categories that would otherwise be tested as competing hypotheses are historically layers of the *same* diagram. A classifier that forces `wind` versus `season` versus `humour` can therefore be conceptually wrong.

### 3.3 BnF Latin 6556: Milanese multi-cycle cosmography

BnF Mandragore identifies Latin 6556 as made in **Milan in the mid-fourteenth century**. Its scientific sequence includes cosmography, a wind rose, zodiacal/cosmographic diagrams, solar and lunar cycles, eclipses, calendars, chronometry and a world map.

Especially important is f.4v, `de circulo XII signorum`, which combines:
- Earth / inhabited world;
- cardinal points;
- the sun's path;
- equinoctial day/night relations;
- the zodiac;
- the four seasonal quarters;
- qualitative labels such as hot/humid.

Ff.6v–7r then use concentric circles for hours, planets and days of the week.

Implication: in fourteenth-century Milan, annular scientific graphics already served as **multi-layer alignment devices** between spatial, temporal, elemental/qualitative and astronomical systems.

### 3.4 `Breviari d'Amor`, Royal MS 19 C I: anthropomorphs can be operators, not semantic labels

The British Library catalogues this early-fourteenth-century southern-French encyclopaedic manuscript as containing:
- f.34v: universe with **two angels turning the axes**;
- f.50r: spheres of elements and planets with **four angels holding them**;
- f.51r: a **separate wind diagram**;
- f.53r: daylight hours;
- f.53v: circular planets/days-of-week diagram;
- f.54v: seasons.

Implication: figures physically interacting with a cosmological circle need not themselves be winds. The same manuscript explicitly distinguishes cosmic operators/holders from a wind diagram.

### 3.5 Isidorian `Mundus–Annus–Homo` tradition: two crossed opposition axes are normal

Walters W.73 f.8r and related Isidorian diagrams correlate four seasons, qualities, cardinal directions, elements and humours. OMCI/INHA describes the Isidorian sublunary mutation diagram as superposing the quaternities of world, year and human in concentric circular form. The Oxford Cabinet commentary emphasises that the scheme reconciles opposites through a circular pattern.

Implication: the f57v N/S versus E/W distinction has a credible **structural** home in paired-opposition cosmology, even though no historical example has yet been found that expresses the exact distinction through the same inward/outward facial orientations and `2-1-2-1` gestures.

### 3.6 Computus and finger reckoning: hands can carry data

Morgan MS M.925, written in northern Italy, possibly Piacenza, 1018–1032, contains Bede's finger-reckoning material. The Morgan catalogue records dozens of hand configurations encoding numbers and male half-figures in medallions whose one hand encodes a number while the other hand points or interacts with the head/body. The same codex contains circular computational, wind and prognostic diagrams, including the Sphere of Petosiris.

Implication: a medieval scientific figure's hand is not automatically an iconographic `gesture`; it can be notation. This is older than f57v, but it creates a specific falsifiable hand-shape hypothesis.

### 3.7 Wheel of Fortune: four axial humans can intentionally be unequal states

Morgan M.48 f.37r (Paris, 1340–50) and M.324 f.34v (Paris, ca.1350) show four men around Fortune's wheel in different states: ascending, reigning, descending and fallen/recumbent.

Implication: positional asymmetry among four humans is a normal information-bearing device in medieval circular imagery. This is a structural control, not a claim that f57v is a Fortune wheel.

## 5. Why integrated tetradic cosmology now outranks the single-subject models

Three independent observations converge:

1. **Historical diagrams bundle the candidate semantics.** Cambridge Ff.2.6, Wellcome MS225 and NYPL MA069 explicitly correlate winds with seasons, elements, humours, directions, ages and qualities rather than separating them.
2. **The rings are likely primary information-bearing structures.** Multi-layer cosmographic and computistical diagrams use concentric rings to align periodic systems, exactly the architectural function that f57v's multiple textual annuli could support.
3. **The four people need not be four equivalent nouns.** Royal MS 19 C I and Fortune wheels show that human figures can operate, hold, move, index or represent different states around circular systems.

This umbrella model also explains why the search for a single exact iconographic comparator has stalled: f57v may be a transformed concordance rather than a copy of one canonical image type.

## 6. The N/S vs E/W polarity

Medieval Aristotelian/Isidorian systems are fundamentally built from **paired oppositions** (hot/cold and wet/dry), and quaternity diagrams commonly project such relations onto crossed axes. This makes the opposed-axis architecture meaningful rather than anomalous.

However, no qualified source found in Phases 3–4 maps the specific f57v pattern `N/S outward + 2 gestures; E/W inward + 1 gesture` directly onto hot/cold, wet/dry, elements, winds, seasons or humours. It would be an overclaim to assign those semantics now.

A related Aristotelian fact is that earth/water were understood as heavy and naturally centre-seeking while air/fire were light and naturally outward/rising. This demonstrates that **inward/outward polarity itself can be scientifically meaningful**, but the standard four-element topology does not automatically yield the exact f57v opposed-pair layout. Treat this as a testable clue, not an identification.

## 7. Decisive empirical programme

### T1 — Gesture-to-ring registration
For each secure hand, derive a ray from forearm/hand direction, intersect every annular baseline/ring boundary and measure proximity to:
- token boundaries;
- unusually large gaps;
- repeated glyph clusters;
- radial discontinuities/dividers;
- aligned boundaries on adjacent rings.

Null: rotate the complete six-ray gesture field through many random angles while preserving relative geometry. The operator/index model predicts an excess of meaningful registrations over the rotation null.

### T2 — Opposed-axis textual contrast
Partition each ring into N/S-class versus E/W-class sectors using frozen angular windows. Compute token/glyph n-gram distributions, entropy, positional features and transition profiles. Test whether within-class similarity (N~S and E~W) exceeds across-class similarity under sector-label permutation.

The paired-opposition model predicts a reproducible two-class signal.

### T3 — Cross-ring phase locking
Detect word/gap/divider boundaries on each annulus and test angular alignment across radii. A multi-layer concordance predicts more cross-ring phase locking than independent circular prose would.

### T4 — Periodicity scan
Test angular/textual periodicities at k=4, 8, 12 and related harmonics. A quaternity concordance layered with months/zodiac/winds naturally predicts 4/12 structure; a simple four-person icon need not.

### T5 — Blind hand-shape/dactylonomy coding
Before interpreting fingers as pointing, record visible digit flexion, thumb contact, palm orientation and laterality. Compare to medieval reckoning tables without revealing f57v identity. Reject the reckoning hypothesis if no recognized configurations survive conservative coding.

### T6 — Matched historical panel
Acquire and code, without semantic labels during morphology scoring:

Features: annuli, number of semantic layers, sectorization, human equivalence/non-equivalence, facing, hand morphology, centre relation, cross-ring alignment, explicit directional/breath/medical/astronomical tokens.

## 8. Falsification map

The integrated-tetradic model weakens sharply if:
- annular text lacks any stable 4/8/12 periodicity or cross-ring phase alignment;
- N/S and E/W sectors have no reproducible textual distinction;
- hand vectors show no non-random relation to ring/text anchors;
- a single iconographic interpretation explains all four figures and centre with strong period-near parallels.

The operator model weakens if the hands do not interact with ring structure and adjacent text treats all four positions equivalently.

The medical-humoral subtype weakens if there is no paired-quality/seasonal structure and no relation to known medical diagrammatics.

The wind model regains relative strength if a period-near wind witness is found with the same opposed-facing/gesture structure or if f57v reveals wind-specific residues (breath, instruments, held wind heads, directional nomenclature).

## 9. Current scientific position

**Deep-research conclusion:** the strongest alternative to `f57v = wind rota` is not another isolated iconographic label. It is **f57v = multi-layer tetradic concordance / cosmological-computational rota**, potentially incorporating wind as one correlated layer among world, year, body, direction, season, element, humour and astronomical cycles.

This model has direct medieval precedent, survives into the early fifteenth century, and has a geographically relevant fourteenth-century Milanese scientific context. It also makes concrete predictions about the annular text and pointing geometry that can now be tested quantitatively.

No exact historical match to the full f57v `opposed-facing + 2-1-2-1` human morphology has been found. That remains the central unresolved anomaly.
