# ALTERNATIVES DEEP RESEARCH — f57v

Date: 2026-08-09
Status: exploratory historical synthesis after Phase 3; not a target-fitting preregistration

## Starting constraint

The corrected f57v morphology is treated as fixed:

- page-N: outward-facing, two secure gestures approximately E/W;
- page-E: inward-facing, one secure gesture approximately W;
- page-S: outward-facing, two secure gestures approximately E/W;
- page-W: inward-facing, one secure gesture approximately S;
- circular/concentric text-bearing framework;
- no secure breath streams;
- no secure subordinate wind heads;
- no exact medieval comparator for the opposed-axis 2-1-2-1 posture/gesture signature was found in Phase 3.

This deep-research phase therefore asks what *other medieval diagrammatic roles* can naturally generate four humans in a circular system, differentiated axes, gestures, and annular information.

## Main finding

The strongest alternative is not a single semantic identification such as `elements` or `ages of man`. It is a broader representational family:

> **tetradic correlation / quaternity diagrams**, in which one four-position visual grammar is reused to correlate multiple sets of four — directions, elements, qualities, seasons, humours, winds, stages of life, solar phases, lunar phases — and in which human figures can function either as personifications or as heuristic operators.

This matters because it predicts semantic underdetermination from figure count and placement alone. The same page architecture can carry different subjects.

## 1. Tetradic correlation / encyclopedic quaternity — CURRENT RANK 1

### Cambridge, Gonville & Caius MS 428/428, `Tractatus de quaternario`

The early-12th-century manuscript is the strongest control population located in the search. Elizabeth Sears characterized the anonymous `Tractatus de quaternario` as a handbook of tetradic cosmology. Modern scholarship confirms that its diagrammatic programme correlates stages of life with humours and other natural quaternities; the famous f.28v diagram visualizes four ages as four women.

The wider programme is especially important for f57v because multiple circular/quartered diagrams in the *same manuscript* reuse anthropomorphic and diagrammatic forms for different tetrads. Published discussions identify personifications or correlated sets for directions, elements, humours, seasons, winds, life stages, and astronomical/temporal categories. Obrist specifically notes personifications of the four cardinal directions on f.22r. A published analysis of f.40v identifies four elemental zodiacal triplicities, placing fire, air, water and earth at the four sides rather than simply following zodiacal sequence.

**Implication:** `four axial people in a cosmological circle` is a reusable syntax, not a semantic label. Wind is one legal value of the syntax, but not the default.

**Fit to f57v:** high at family level; exact 2-1-2-1 posture remains unmatched.

## 2. Humans as diagrammatic operators / motion markers — CURRENT RANK 2

### Gossuin de Metz, `L'Image du monde`

Miranda Griffin's 2025 study of the illustrated `Image du monde` tradition provides a qualitatively different explanation for human figures in circles. In Paris, Arsenal 3516 f.164v, human placeholders occur at four equidistant points of concentric-circle diagrams and are labelled `hom qui va` (man who goes). A paired diagram represents figures parting at the top and meeting at the bottom. Cambridge UL Gg.1.1 f.359r uses actual human figures at four equidistant points around the circular Earth.

Griffin explicitly argues that the relevant information is motion and *relative positioning*: the figures are heuristic components of the diagram, not realistically scaled inhabitants. Other witnesses substitute words/calligraphic statements for moving humans. In the same work, a tetradic wheel in Arsenal 3516 f.179r correlates cardinal direction, element, elemental qualities, season, humour and stage of human life in linked circles around a central `annus`, with months around the perimeter.

This is highly relevant to f57v because its figures need not be personifications at all. They could be **operators/indexers/readers** whose facing and hands specify how to traverse, compare, or select portions of the rings.

The tradition was not geographically confined to northern France: BnF Français 2173, an `Image du monde` manuscript made in Venice/Veneto in the third quarter of the 13th century, preserves extensive cosmographic diagrams, including cardinal-direction/world diagrams.

**Implication:** f57v's asymmetry may be functional rather than iconographic. The N/S and E/W figures could perform two different operations on annular material.

**Fit to f57v:** high explanatory fit to asymmetric facing/gestures; no known exact 2-1-2-1 witness.

## 3. Macrocosm–microcosm / paired-opposites medical cosmology — CURRENT RANK 3

### Isidorian and medical quaternity wheels

The INHA/OMCI description of BL Harley MS 3099 f.157 (ca.1130–1174) is a clean structural analogue. Concentric circles combine `mundus`, `annus` and `homo`; fire/water occupy opposite vertical positions and earth/air opposite horizontal positions. Seasons and humours are associated with those elements. The four states are opposed across **two orthogonal axes** and connected through hot/cold and wet/dry qualities.

Walters W.73.7v and 8r independently preserve wheel diagrams correlating elements, humours, seasons, qualities and cardinal directions. This is exactly the medieval scientific environment in which an N/S pair can have one functional relationship and an E/W pair another without implying four equivalent cardinal beings.

A particularly important conceptual point is that these diagrams can be read both as oppositions and as cycles of transformation. The orthogonal cross is therefore not merely spatial; it can encode a relational algebra.

**Implication:** f57v's strongest unexplained feature — its opposed-axis polarity — is native to medieval scientific diagrammatics even though the exact facial orientations are not yet matched.

**Fit to f57v:** high conceptual fit; moderate morphological fit because standard examples often use labels/arcs rather than four people.

## 4. Solar-course / time-of-day / life-cycle diagram — CURRENT RANK 4

### Francesco da Barberino and Padua

Andrea von Hülsen-Esch's 2026 study of Francesco da Barberino's `Officiolum` is important chronologically and geographically. Francesco was in Padua in 1306–1309 while the work was being created. Its life-stage images deliberately correlate human age with the course of the sun and the hours of prayer; the relation is created by the *visual diagram*, not merely repeated from text. The circular setting invokes planetary orbits and temporal cycles.

The same study notes earlier diagrams in which a human figure is surrounded by concentric circles correlating elements, ages and seasons. Francesco's innovation shows that medieval artists could use the direction/location of human figures and the sun to encode temporal progression and social/biological stages.

This alternative is strengthened by later Paduan evidence. Guariento's mid-14th-century Eremitani cycle combined planets with ages of man. Oxford Bodleian Canon. Misc. 554 was copied in Padua in 1435 by Candus, nephew of Prosdocimo de Beldomandi, and contains constellation/planet drawings partly copied from the Eremitani frescoes. Thus a visual programme linking astronomy/astrology to human states was active in Padua inside the Voynich parchment window.

**Implication:** f57v may encode temporal stages, solar motion, or a life-cycle/medical correlation rather than spatial winds.

**Fit to f57v:** moderate-high context fit; exact four-figure opposed-facing layout unverified.

## 5. Elemental zodiacal triplicity / astrological quaternity — CURRENT RANK 5

Four zodiacal triplicities provide a natural `4 × 3 = 12` architecture independent of twelve winds. A published analysis of Caius 428 f.40v describes the zodiac signs grouped by elemental triplicity rather than sequential zodiacal order, with four elemental groups placed at four sides of the diagram.

This is structurally relevant because a diagram with four principal sectors can control twelve subsidiary units without representing winds. More generally, four elements, four qualities and zodiacal influence are repeatedly linked in medieval medical/astrological diagrammatics.

**Against:** f57v does not currently show secure zodiac glyphs, planet symbols, or an independently demonstrated 4×3 subdivision. This stays below the more generic tetradic and operator models.

## 6. Hybrid synoptic cosmography — CURRENT RANK 6

### Opicinus de Canistris

Opicinus's Pal. lat. 1993 (Avignon, 1335–1353) demonstrates how aggressively late-medieval diagram makers could superimpose heterogeneous systems. The Metropolitan Museum's description of f.24r notes more than twenty coordinated sets — zodiac, months, days, implied world map, personifications of Church, Evangelists, monastic orders, exegetical categories, and others. Karl Whittington and later work stress that the maps/bodies/diagrams combine cartography, allegory, empiricism and cosmology rather than belonging to one simple iconographic class.

This is not a close visual match to f57v. Its value is methodological: a difficult circular diagram can be a **synthetic instrument**, so forcing all f57v features into a single semantic category may be the wrong model.

**Fit:** low-moderate morphology; high warning value against single-label interpretation.

## 7. Wind / anemology — CURRENT RANK 7 AS A SPECIFIC SEMANTIC IDENTIFICATION

Wind remains historically viable and is a major member of the same quaternity/cosmography family. Phases 1–2 established secure medieval precedents for four principal winds, 4+8 hierarchies, anthropomorphic winds and late survival of wind-personification vocabulary.

But Phase 3 found no comparator reproducing f57v's opposed-axis facing and 2-1-2-1 gesture count. Walters W.73 is a particularly clean contrary case: all wind busts blow toward the central Earth. f57v has no secure breath streams, wind instruments or wind labels.

**Implication:** wind should no longer be the prior/default explanation. It remains a branch to test if the internal ring structure produces directional/wind-specific residue.

## 8. Four Rivers of Paradise / theological quaternity — CURRENT RANK 8

Troyes MS 252 f.14 remains the strongest morphology hard negative: four axial male river personifications in a radial theological diagram each hold two medallions, creating a strong `four principals + subordinate round objects` architecture. However, its gesture count is effectively 2-2-2-2, not f57v's 2-1-2-1.

The broader tradition proves that radial four-person syntax can migrate between cosmological and theological meanings. The absence on f57v of water vessels, streams or explicit Paradise iconography makes a direct river identification weak.

## 9. Prognostic / onomantic sphere — CURRENT RANK 9

Late-medieval `Sphaera Pythagorae` / Sphere of Life and Death traditions provide a strong *mechanism* analogue for circular, concentric, rule-governed diagrams used in medicine/divination. Italian mid-14th-century and German mid-15th-century manuscript witnesses exist.

However, the typical operational content is numerical/name-based and does not require four axial human figures. This is therefore a mechanism alternative, not a close iconographic alternative.

## 10. Lower-ranked alternatives

- **Wheel of Fortune / cyclic social-state diagram:** four humans can represent successive states around a wheel, but the central Fortune/ascending-descending semantics do not match f57v well.
- **Lunar phase / computus:** Caius 428 and other scientific manuscripts use fourfold lunar/temporal structures, but f57v lacks secure crescent/disc tokens.
- **Geomancy:** strong paired quality/element/day-night logic but normally encoded through dot figures rather than anthropomorphic axial persons.
- **Pure cardinal-direction personification:** historically attested, including Caius 428 f.22r, but does not explain the 2+2 functional differentiation.

## Most important conceptual revision

The deep search changes the question from:

> `Which four things are the four people?`

to two competing representational hypotheses:

### P — Personification model
The four people *are* four entities: winds, elements, humours, seasons, ages, directions, etc.

### O — Operator model
The four people show *how to read or transform the diagram*: direction of traversal, comparison of opposite sectors, selection of annular spans, or relative position/motion.

The corrected f57v asymmetry makes O newly serious. Four equivalent personifications generally predict stronger rotational equivalence than is actually drawn; operators can legitimately have different orientations and numbers of gestures because they perform different actions.

This is an inference from the historical comparator population, not a historical identification of f57v.

## What the deep search did NOT find

Despite widening beyond wind iconography, the search still found **no independently verified medieval diagram with the exact f57v signature**:

`four axial anthropomorphs + N/S outward + E/W inward + secure 2-1-2-1 gesture count`.

That feature therefore remains potentially diagnostic. It should not be normalized away by restoring imagined missing arms or forcing a fourfold symmetry.

## Decisive next test

The highest-value discriminator is internal rather than another iconographic search:

1. freeze the six secure gesture vectors exactly as currently coded;
2. geometrically extend each gesture vector through the annuli;
3. segment ring boundaries, gaps, radial separators, repeated token anchors and other structural landmarks **without using the gesture positions**;
4. test whether the observed gesture/ring intersections hit high-salience landmarks more often than rotation/permutation nulls;
5. separately test ring periodicity for 4, 8, 12 and other repeated sectors.

Prediction:
- significant gesture-to-text/ring registration raises the **operator/indexing** model sharply;
- clean two-axis opposition without pointer registration raises **macrocosm-microcosm / qualities**;
- 4×3 or 12-fold internal structure with directional residue raises **astrological triplicity or wind**;
- no structural registration raises the likelihood that the humans are conventional personifications or decoration.
