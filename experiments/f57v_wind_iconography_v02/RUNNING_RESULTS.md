# RUNNING RESULTS — f57v Wind Iconography v0.2
Date: 2026-08-09

## Current endpoint status

### E1 — Historical existence: PASS
Multiple independently attested medieval wind witnesses instantiate the compound grammar of four principal winds in a circular/cosmological framework with materially active hands/arms and a subordinate-wind hierarchy.

Strongest examples:
- Venice, Biblioteca Marciana, MS lat. VIII.22 (2760), fol. **2r** (`Astronomy of Nemrod`, ca. 1200): Draelants identifies this folio as `Four cosmic winds`; Obrist describes the four cardinal winds standing on the airy stratum of concentric rings around the earth, with outstretched hands holding collateral-wind heads; the associated textual hierarchy is four principals plus eight collaterals = twelve. Earlier notes incorrectly gave the folio as 2v; this is corrected here to 2r.
- Cambridge, Gonville & Caius College Library, MS 428, fol. 39v (11th–12th c.): Raff describes four principal winds as full figures holding the eight collateral winds as winged heads in their hands.
- Heidelberg, UB, Cod. Sal. X,16, fol. 2v (`Scivias`, ca. 1195–1225): four winged principal figures participate in a circular annual/cosmological scheme and hold subordinate wind heads in their extended hands.

### E2 — Exact hierarchy: PASS
The exact 4 + 8 = 12 representational hierarchy, with four principal figures holding subordinate wind heads, is independently attested. Cambridge MS 428 f.39v and the Nemrod tradition are sufficient to pass this endpoint. Twelve equal visible figures are therefore not required by the historical wind grammar.

### E3 — Chronological bridge: PARTIAL BRIDGE — DIRECT LINEAGE TRACED TO ca. 1330; EXACT HELD-HEAD MORPHOLOGY STILL OPEN
The exact hand-held-head compound is securely attested in the 11th–early 13th centuries. The subsequent evidence now supports transmission by **iconographic transformation**, rather than abrupt disappearance:

- **Turin, Biblioteca Civica, Bosio B.176, fol. 3v, end 13th c.**: Draelants identifies this folio as `Four cosmic winds` in another complete `Liber Nemroth` witness. The Turin library reports a catalogue attribution of the manuscript to a Franciscan and to a Florentine or Sienese milieu. This supplies a potentially important Italian continuation of the Nemrod tradition, but the exact held-collateral-head morphology of fol. 3v has not yet been independently verified from pixels and is therefore coded `morphology unverified`.
- **Metropolitan Museum, Austria, ca. 1300, `Four Winds Diagram`**: the official catalogue describes a rota with four heads in roundels personifying the principal winds and visible blasts, embedded in a cosmological/natural-science diagram. This independently proves persistence of the four-principal axial wind rota around 1300, although it is not an exact held-head copy.
- **Isolated ca. 1300 Petrus-Pictaviensis-related leaf**: Raff records four principal wind heads plus two subordinate wind personifications in each surrounding sector, retaining a 4 + 8 hierarchy but changing the subordinate morphology to instrument-bearing figures. Because the leaf's codicological provenance is lost, it is quarantined from geographic inference.
- **Munich, BSB Clm 2660, ca. 1300, Aldersbach**: a related Petrus Pictaviensis tradition retains the diagrammatic genealogy/world-scheme but its characteristic early folios are drawn schematically without the same figural wind apparatus. This is evidence for abstraction within the same broader transmission environment, not evidence of extinction.
- **Linz, Cod. 490, Baumgartenberg tradition**: Hranitzky describes a disc-shaped world scheme organized in rings and structured by twelve winds, with four principal winds represented crosswise/cardinally as old male heads.
- **Vienna, ÖNB Cod. 364, fol. 4v, Klosterneuburg ca. 1330**: Hranitzky establishes direct copying from the Linz/Baumgartenberg tradition. Crucially, the Klosterneuburg illuminator deliberately reinterpreted the smaller figures with water vessels as personifications of the four Paradise rivers and halved their number, while retaining inherited horns and wind names in the inscriptions; the principal winds were redrawn as young men. The modern ÖNB manifest independently catalogues fol. 4v as containing personifications of both `Paradiesflüsse` and `Winde`.

This sequence materially narrows the chronological gap. It demonstrates a transmitted four-principal / subordinate-wind world-diagram lineage continuing through ca. 1300–1330 while changing morphology and semantics. It does **not** yet establish an unchanged `four principal full figures physically holding eight heads` witness in 1400–1450.

### E4 — Late-medieval component continuity: PASS
The wind-personification / circular-wind vocabulary clearly survives into and beyond the Voynich period. Relevant continuity includes:

- **Gregorio Dati, `La sfera`, Florence, composed ca. 1420–1430**: Book III explicitly structures geographical description around lands, seas and winds; the work was conceived as text accompanied by diagrams/images and survives in a large illustrated manuscript tradition. This is contemporaneous conceptual continuity inside the Voynich radiocarbon window. Current verification distinguishes the composition date from the individual surviving illustrated witnesses: the securely inspected illustrated copies in this programme are around the mid-15th century rather than proven autograph-era copies.
- **Morgan MS M.721, Florence, second half 15th c.**: official catalogue identifies a diagram of four winds personified as faces and a separate wheel with wind names.
- **NYPL MA 109 / MssCol 2557, 15th c.**: four winds personified in a miniature.
- **Bodleian MS Bodl. 646, Padua, ca. 1460**: scholarly catalogue describes fol. 34r as a table with twelve blowing wind heads.
- **Vienna ÖNB MS 216, fol. 49r, second half 15th c., probably Verona according to Raff**: a Sallust/Ptolemy world map surrounded by twelve differentiated wind heads, all blowing into curved horns. Modern catalogue dating/provenance remains to be reconciled independently.

Thus the chronological objection now applies only to survival of the **exact hand-held subordinate-head device**, not to wind personification, axial principal winds, 4 + 8 / twelve-wind hierarchy, or circular wind schemata as such.

### E5 — Geographic sensitivity: PASS for component continuity; PARTIALLY BRIDGED for transmission; OPEN for exact compound
The corridor is now stronger than a generic `Italy` claim:
- end-13th-century Turin Bosio B.176 is attributed in the Turin catalogue to a Florentine or Sienese milieu, while its exact morphology remains unverified;
- ca. 1300–1330 transformed wind-world descendants are documented in Austria/Bavaria;
- Florence has a live wind-cosmography tradition in Dati's `La sfera` from ca. 1420–1430;
- later-15th-century Florence, Padua, and probably Verona preserve anthropomorphic wind diagrams.

The exact principal-figure + held-subordinate-head compound has still not been verified in northern Italy or the Alpine corridor in 1400–1450.

## Current scientific position
The historical-impossibility objection is falsified. Medieval wind iconography supplies a specific grammar in which four axial/principal anthropomorphs, concentric cosmological organization, active hands/arms, and a 4 + 8 = 12 wind hierarchy coexist.

The chronological evidence now favors a **transformed schematic descendant / shared diagrammatic grammar hypothesis** over a literal direct-copy hypothesis. The same family can preserve hierarchy while changing full figures to heads, heads to instrument-bearing figures, wind figures to partly reinterpreted river figures, or figural schemes to abstract diagrams.

What remains unproved is whether f57v instantiates that transmitted grammar and whether an unchanged held-head compound survived into the 1400–1450 northern-Italian/Alpine environment. The next search is therefore restricted to that exact chronological/geographical bridge and to non-wind fourfold rota controls; generic medieval circles are no longer informative.
