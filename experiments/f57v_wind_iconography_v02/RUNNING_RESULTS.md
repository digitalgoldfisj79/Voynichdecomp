# RUNNING RESULTS — f57v Wind Iconography v0.2
Date: 2026-08-09

## Current endpoint status

### E1 — Historical existence: PASS
Multiple independently attested medieval wind witnesses instantiate the compound grammar of four principal winds in a circular/cosmological framework with materially active hands/arms and a subordinate-wind hierarchy.

Strongest examples:
- Venice, Biblioteca Marciana, MS lat. 2760, fol. 2v (`Astronomy of Nemrod`, ca. 1200): four cardinal winds stand on the airy stratum of concentric rings around the earth; their outstretched hands hold collateral-wind heads; associated text explicitly gives four principals plus eight collaterals = twelve.
- Cambridge, Gonville & Caius College Library, MS 428, fol. 39v (11th–12th c.): Raff describes four principal winds as full figures holding the eight collateral winds as winged heads in their hands.
- Heidelberg, UB, Cod. Sal. X,16, fol. 2v (`Scivias`, ca. 1195–1225): four winged principal figures participate in a circular annual/cosmological scheme and hold subordinate wind heads in their extended hands.

### E2 — Exact hierarchy: PASS
The exact 4 + 8 = 12 representational hierarchy, with four principal figures holding subordinate wind heads, is independently attested. Cambridge MS 428 f.39v and the Nemrod tradition are sufficient to pass this endpoint.

### E3 — Chronological bridge: OPEN / PARTIALLY BRIDGED
The exact hand-held-head compound is securely attested around the 11th–early 13th centuries. Raff documents transformed descendants around 1300–1335:
- an isolated ca. 1300 leaf preserves four principal medallion heads with two subordinate wind personifications in each surrounding sector, but its codicological provenance is lost and it is therefore quarantined from a strong bridge claim;
- Vienna, ÖNB, MS 364, fol. 4v (ca. 1335) preserves a modified fourfold wind scheme whose copied inscriptions still refer to eight winds even though the image has been rationalized into four winds plus four rivers.

No securely verified 1400–1450 witness has yet been found that preserves the *exact* principal-figures-holding-eight-heads device. This remains the main open discriminant.

### E4 — Late-medieval component continuity: PASS
The wind-personification / circular-wind vocabulary clearly survives into and beyond the Voynich period. Relevant continuity includes:
- Gregorio Dati's `La sfera`, composed ca. 1420–1430 in Florence, whose Book III explicitly structures geography around winds and whose illustrated manuscript tradition contains wind diagrams/personifications;
- Morgan MS M.721, Florence, second half of the 15th c.: official catalogue identifies a diagram of four winds personified as faces and a separate wheel with wind names;
- NYPL MA 109 / MssCol 2557, 15th c.: four winds personified in a miniature;
- Bodleian MS Bodl. 646, Padua, ca. 1460: scholarly catalogue describes fol. 34r as a table with twelve blowing wind heads;
- Raff's later-15th-century Sallust/Ptolemy witness, Vienna ÖNB MS 216 fol. 49r, described as a world map surrounded by twelve differentiated wind heads; Raff dates it to the second half of the 15th c., probably Verona (modern catalogue dating still requires reconciliation).

Thus the chronological objection applies specifically to the *exact hand-held subordinate-head morphology*, not to medieval wind personification or circular wind diagrams in general.

### E5 — Geographic sensitivity: PASS for component continuity; OPEN for exact compound
Northern/central Italian continuity is independently attested in Florence, Padua, and probably Verona. The exact B+E+F hand-held-head compound has not yet been verified in northern Italy/Alpine regions near 1400–1450.

## f57v comparison frozen independently
Observed on f57v:

- axial/cardinal placement: present;
- centre-related extended arms/hands: present at ensemble level;
- clearly held subordinate heads: not observed;
- explicit 4+8=12 semantics: unknown;

## Current scientific position
The historical-impossibility objection is no longer tenable. Medieval wind iconography does provide a specific representational grammar in which four axial/principal anthropomorphs, concentric cosmological organization, hand/arm gestures, and a twelve-wind hierarchy coexist.

What remains unproved is whether f57v actually instantiates that grammar, and whether the exact compound remained available in the fifteenth-century northern-Italian/Alpine manuscript environment. The next search is therefore narrowly targeted at the 1300–1450 chronological bridge rather than generic wind diagrams.
