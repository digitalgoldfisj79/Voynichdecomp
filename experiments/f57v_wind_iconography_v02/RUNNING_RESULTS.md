# RUNNING RESULTS — f57v Wind Iconography v0.2
Date: 2026-08-09

## Current endpoint status

### E1 — Historical existence: PASS
Multiple independently attested medieval wind witnesses instantiate the compound grammar of four principal winds in a circular/cosmological framework with materially active hands/arms and a subordinate-wind hierarchy.

Strongest examples:
- Venice, Biblioteca Marciana, MS lat. VIII.22 (2760), fol. **2r** (`Astronomy of Nemrod`, ca. 1200): Draelants identifies this folio as `Four cosmic winds`; Obrist describes the four cardinal winds standing on the airy stratum of concentric rings around the earth, with outstretched hands holding collateral-wind heads; the associated textual hierarchy is four principals plus eight collaterals = twelve. Earlier notes incorrectly gave the folio as 2v; this is corrected here to 2r.
- Cambridge, Gonville & Caius College Library, MS 428, fol. 39v (11th–12th c.): Raff describes four principal winds as full figures holding the eight collateral winds as winged heads in their hands.
- Heidelberg, UB, Cod. Sal. X,16, fol. 2v (`Scivias`, ca. 1195–1225): four winged principal figures participate in a circular annual/cosmological scheme and hold subordinate wind heads in their extended hands.

### E2 — Exact hierarchy: PASS
The exact 4 + 8 = 12 representational hierarchy, with four principal figures holding subordinate wind heads, is independently attested. Cambridge MS 428 f.39v and the Nemrod tradition are sufficient to pass this endpoint. Twelve equal visible figures are therefore not required by the historical wind grammar.

### E3 — Chronological bridge: SUBSTANTIALLY BRIDGED AT COMPONENT LEVEL; EXACT 4+8 HELD-HEAD ROTA STILL OPEN
The exact hand-held-head compound is securely attested in the 11th–early 13th centuries. The subsequent evidence now supports transmission by **iconographic transformation**, rather than abrupt disappearance:

- **Turin, Biblioteca Civica, Bosio B.176, fol. 3v, end 13th c.**: Draelants identifies this folio as `Four cosmic winds` in another complete `Liber Nemroth` witness. The Turin library reports a catalogue attribution of the manuscript to a Franciscan and to a Florentine or Sienese milieu. Exact held-collateral-head morphology remains unverified from pixels.
- **Metropolitan Museum, Austria, ca. 1300, `Four Winds Diagram`**: four heads in roundels personify principal winds in a cosmological/natural-science rota.
- **Isolated ca. 1300 Petrus-Pictaviensis-related leaf**: Raff records four principal wind heads plus two subordinate wind personifications in each surrounding sector, retaining 4 + 8 while changing subordinate morphology to instrument-bearing figures. Provenance is lost and quarantined.
- **Munich, BSB Clm 2660, ca. 1300, Aldersbach**: a related Petrus Pictaviensis transmission abstracts the figural apparatus into a more schematic diagram.
- **Linz Cod. 490 -> Vienna ÖNB Cod. 364 f.4v, ca.1330**: Hranitzky establishes direct copying in which a twelve-wind/four-principal world scheme is transformed. In Vienna the smaller figures are reinterpreted as four Paradise rivers and reduced in number while inherited horns and wind names persist.
- **British Library Yates Thompson MS 10 f.11v, ca.1370–1390, Paris**: official catalogue states that four angels **hold the winds, symbolised by four animal heads**, at the four corners of the earth. This is not a 4+8 rota, but it decisively demonstrates survival of the `detached wind head physically handled by an anthropomorphic agent` token into the late 14th century.
- **Giusto de' Menabuoi, Padua Baptistery, 1375–1378**: the securely dated Padua Apocalypse cycle contains a surviving image independently catalogued as `Four winds`. A dedicated pixel-only model audit reads the winds as disembodied heads physically held/blocked by the angels' hands. The scene/date/geography are secure; the exact hand/head morphology is currently machine-audited rather than catalogue-described and is therefore treated as strong auxiliary evidence.

This now bridges the crucial *component morphology* to within roughly one generation of the Voynich window in **Padua itself**. It still does **not** establish an unchanged `four principal full figures each holding two subordinate heads` rota in 1400–1450.

### E4 — Late-medieval component continuity: PASS
The wind-personification / circular-wind vocabulary clearly survives into and beyond the Voynich period. Relevant continuity includes:

- **Gregorio Dati, `La sfera`, Florence, composed ca. 1420–1430**: Book III explicitly structures geography around lands, seas and winds; the work was conceived as text accompanied by diagrams/images and survives in a large illustrated manuscript tradition. Composition falls inside the Voynich radiocarbon window; securely inspected illustrated witnesses in this programme are around the mid-15th century rather than autograph-era copies.
- **Konrad Kyeser, `Bellifortis`, Besançon BM MS 1360 f.4v, Germany, 1400–1449**: Biblissima/Initiale identifies the image as `Vents, Soleil et Lune`. This supplies full anthropomorphic wind imagery integrated with celestial/atmospheric imagery inside the Voynich window, although it is not a rota and not a held-head 4+8 scheme.
- **Galatina, Santa Caterina, Apocalypse cycle, first half 15th c.**: Raff places this Italian cycle in the late `restraining the winds` tradition and describes wind heads; modern cataloguing independently verifies the cycle/date/location, but exact-panel pixel verification remains pending.
- **Morgan MS M.721, Florence, second half 15th c.**: official catalogue identifies four winds personified as faces and a separate wind-name wheel.
- **NYPL MA 109 / MssCol 2557, 15th c.**: four winds personified in a miniature.
- **Bodleian MS Bodl. 646, Padua, ca.1460**: scholarly catalogue describes fol. 34r as a table with twelve blowing wind heads.
- **Vienna ÖNB MS 216, fol.49r, second half 15th c., probably Verona according to Raff**: a Sallust/Ptolemy world map surrounded by twelve differentiated wind heads, all blowing into curved horns.

Thus a broad claim that wind personification or hand/wind-head imagery was obsolete by 1400 is no longer tenable. The chronological question applies only to survival of the **exact early 4+8 held-head rota morphology**.

### E5 — Geographic sensitivity: STRONGLY BRIDGED FOR COMPONENTS; OPEN FOR EXACT 4+8 COMPOUND
The corridor is now more specific:
- end-13th-century Turin Bosio B.176 is attributed to a Florentine or Sienese milieu, exact morphology pending;
- ca.1300–1330 transformed wind-world descendants are documented in Austria/Bavaria;
- **Padua 1375–1378** supplies a geographically direct four-winds / hand-control bridge;
- Florence has a live wind-cosmography programme in Dati's `La sfera` from ca.1420–1430;
- first-half-15th-century German `Bellifortis` preserves anthropomorphic winds inside the VMS window;
- later-15th-century Florence, Padua, and probably Verona preserve anthropomorphic wind diagrams.

The exact principal-figure + two-held-subordinate-heads-per-principal compound has still not been verified in northern Italy or the Alpine corridor in 1400–1450.

## Current scientific position
The historical-impossibility objection is falsified. Medieval wind iconography supplies a specific grammar in which four axial/principal anthropomorphs, concentric cosmological organization, active hands/arms, and a 4 + 8 = 12 wind hierarchy coexist.

The chronological evidence now favors a **transformed schematic descendant / shared diagrammatic grammar hypothesis** over a literal direct-copy hypothesis. Critically, the two most f57v-relevant component vocabularies are independently shown to persist much later than the earliest exact examples:
1. four-principal / circular / twelve-wind cosmographic architecture;
2. detached wind heads physically handled or controlled by human/angelic figures.

Their historical persistence makes recombination or transformed descent plausible. It does not identify f57v as a wind diagram.

The programme's main falsification target now changes: instead of collecting more generic wind examples, build a matched 1300–1450 **non-wind** corpus with exactly four axial anthropomorphs, repeated centre-directed hand gestures, concentric annuli and a central emblem. If that conjunction is common outside wind/cosmography, the f57v hypothesis loses specificity.
