# Raff full-text audit — findings relevant to f57v
Date: 2026-08-09
Source: Thomas Raff, `Die Ikonografie der mittelalterlichen Windpersonifikation`, Aachener Kunstblätter 48 (1978–79), 71–218.

The complete Raff article was retrieved from the Heidelberg journal archive and read programmatically page by page. The statements below record attested iconographic structures; they are not prevalence estimates.

## 1. Four principal winds can stand on the axes of a circle
Raff explicitly discusses representations in which four heads or figures occupy the vertical and horizontal axes of a circular world image to denote the four cardinal directions/winds. This is a direct precedent for treating an axial four-person arrangement as wind-compatible rather than generically cosmological only.

## 2. Four full figures can encode twelve winds by holding subordinate heads
Raff describes a recurring hierarchy in which the principal winds are full figures and collateral winds are reduced to heads. Especially relevant examples include:

- Cambridge, Gonville & Caius College Library, MS 428, fol. 39v: a wind scheme with four principal winds as full figures holding the eight collateral winds as winged heads in their hands (4 + 8 = 12). The Warburg Institute independently identifies the surviving object as MS 428, fol. 39v–40r, an English circular astronomical/astrological diagram of the 11th–12th century.
- Heidelberg, Universitätsbibliothek, Cod. Sal. X,16, fol. 2v (`Liber Scivias`, ca. 1195–1225): the official Heidelberg description states that four winds appear again in the corner zones as naked winged figures and each holds a collateral-wind head in its outstretched hands. The same page is a complex circular annual/cosmological scheme.
- Raff also documents further variants where principal figures hold or control wind heads and where rank is encoded by full figure versus head/bust.

This establishes that `four visible principal anthropomorphs` is fully compatible with an underlying twelve-wind system. Twelve equal figures are not required.

## 3. The main/collateral hierarchy is structurally stable
Raff repeatedly documents twelve-sector or twelve-agent arrangements in which four principal winds are graphically distinguished from eight collateral winds by size, frontal/profile orientation, wings, instruments, or reduction to heads. Thus 4+8 is not an ad-hoc reconstruction but a conventional wind hierarchy.

## 4. Four axial figures with extended arms are independently attested
Raff describes a circular scheme in which four naked men stand on the horizontal and vertical axes around a central circle with outstretched arms, with smaller subordinate figures around them. This is morphologically relevant to f57v even where the exact semantic role of the f57v hands remains uncertain.

## 5. Fifteenth-century continuity
The exact `four full figures holding eight heads` examples identified above are substantially earlier than the Voynich radiocarbon window. This chronological gap must not be minimized. However Raff's catalogue shows that wind personification itself continues through the 14th and 15th centuries, including late-medieval manuscript and monumental examples, and notes renewed prominence of anthropomorphic winds in later-15th-century antiquarian/classical imagery.

Examples explicitly discussed by Raff include fifteenth-century wind heads in manuscript illumination and a fifteenth-century Sallust/Ptolemy map surrounded by twelve differentiated wind heads. Therefore the iconographic vocabulary survives; what remains to be demonstrated is continuity of the *exact compound grammar* into ca. 1400–1450.

## 6. Implication for f57v
The following claim is now falsified as a historical-impossibility claim:

> A circular diagram with four anthropomorphs at cardinal/axial positions, arm/hand gestures, and an underlying twelve-direction wind hierarchy has no medieval wind-iconographic precedent.

There are multiple independent precedents for its constituent combination and at least two for the especially diagnostic principal-figures-plus-subordinate-heads device.

This does **not** identify f57v as a wind diagram. The decisive unresolved issues are:
- whether the f57v hands encode directional/subordinate relations or merely decorative gestures;
- whether the central six-lobed object has atmospheric/cosmological significance;
- whether the exact four-principal / eight-collateral grammar can be documented close to 1400–1450;
- whether a matched control population shows that this conjunction is materially more wind-specific than generic medieval rota imagery.
