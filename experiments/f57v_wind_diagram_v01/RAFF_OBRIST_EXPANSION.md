## Why this matters
Thomas Raff, *Die Ikonografie der mittelalterlichen Windpersonifikation*, Aachener Kunstblätter 48 (1978-79), pp. 71-218, is a dedicated catalogue/study of medieval wind personifications. Barbara Obrist explicitly identifies Raff's catalogue as a useful guide while noting that it is incomplete because non-personified diagrammatic winds are more numerous.

Obrist's discussion of the *Astronomy of Nemrod* supplies an especially relevant morphological comparator. In Venice, Biblioteca Marciana, MS lat. 2760, fol. 2v (ca. 1200), four cardinal winds stand on the airy stratum of concentric rings around the earth; each extends its hands and holds the heads of collateral winds. The accompanying system is four cardinal winds plus two collateral winds for each cardinal = twelve. This establishes that a four-agent radial arrangement can encode twelve wind directions through hand-held/hand-indicated subordinate heads rather than through twelve equal primary figures.

The Nemrod case is therefore a specific control against the claim that a four-figure central composition cannot belong to a twelve-wind tradition. It is not chronologically close to the Voynich manuscript and cannot by itself establish a fifteenth-century relationship.

## v0.2 population question

> Within the documented population of medieval wind personifications, how frequent is the conjunction of (i) four principal anthropomorphs, (ii) concentric/radial cosmological structure, (iii) hand/arm indication of subordinate directions or objects, and (iv) an implicit or explicit 4+8=12 organization?

This requires exhaustive extraction of Raff's catalogue entries, date/place coding, and image acquisition where extant. The resulting frequency estimate is separate from v0.1 and must not retroactively tune its scoring features.
