# Recovery Is Not Recognition v0.6 Revision Audit

## Decision

Version 0.6 is a substantive reframing of v0.5.2. Experimental outcomes and decision boundaries are unchanged. The revision corrects an asymmetric literature review that risked presenting successful historical decipherment as a strawman and treating Voynich as representative of the field.

## Argumentative correction

The paper now begins with positive recovery. Egyptian hieroglyphs, Linear B, Copiale, Mary Stuart's correspondence, and Zodiac 340 establish document-wide recovery, prediction, and corroboration as attainable standards. The Royal Game of Ur demonstrates operational reconstruction; Rohonc supplies an intermediate case; Linear A, Indus signs, and Voynich illustrate distinct forms of low-anchor uncertainty. The five evidential levels are presented as an operational computational vocabulary derived from these practices, not as standards invented by the paper.

## EVA correction

EVA is expanded and defined on first substantive use as the Extensible Voynich Alphabet. The paper states that it is Voynich-specific, editorial, convenient rather than phonetic, and one of several possible transcription systems. Functional parallels are identified—diplomatic and interpretive transcription, EpiDoc, GORILA-derived identifiers, and numbered sign catalogues—without treating them as equivalent alphabets.

## Assumption audit

- `Low-anchor` is explicitly identified as the paper's operational shorthand.
- `Charged` is defined as accounting for or withholding analyst-supplied selection freedom.
- The cipher portfolio is justified as a coverage panel spanning distinct inference burdens.
- Thresholds are described as calibrated or prospective operational rules rather than universal constants.
- Principal fixed choices and their inferential consequences are recorded in Supplementary Table S4.
- A roadmap explains why each experiment appears and which evidential transition it tests.
