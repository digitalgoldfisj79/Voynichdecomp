# Recovery Is Not Recognition v0.5 Consolidated Revision Audit

## Decision

Version 0.5 is a controlled conceptual rewrite of v0.4.1. The revision is complete at manuscript-build level and is suitable for external review. It is not submission-frozen because clean-room recomputation, reviewer-archive assembly, inherited reference completion, and possible length reduction remain outstanding.

## Scientific correction

The former generator section placed too much evidential weight on one four-part PGCS representation and an aggregate 84-metric pass count. Version 0.5 corrects both issues. PGCS is now one non-unique operational segmentation within an established slot-analysis tradition. Later charged representation comparisons are reported as rejecting the strong claim that four surface slots are intrinsically optimal.

The 84-metric panel is now explained rather than merely named. Its published and extended components remain useful diagnostics, but adversarial controls falsified the aggregate count as a fidelity measure. The manuscript therefore distinguishes selected-statistic compatibility from held-out distributional adequacy. C2ST and the focused H2 order/diversity test are integrated with their proper bounded scope.

## Constructive mechanisms and provenance

The paper now states the asymmetric evidential value of forward models. A fitted generator or manually executable cipher can show that selected observables are possible under a mechanism. It can refute statistical-uniqueness claims. It does not, without independent recognition and attribution evidence, show that the historical target was produced by that mechanism. Naibbe is treated as a fair external example of that distinction rather than as a target of criticism.

## Transliteration boundary

EVA is treated as a measurement model. Character, token, slot, sequence, and classifier results are formally claims about a specified transliteration pipeline. Robustness across shared EVA-derived files does not by itself bound uncertainty arising from the shared glyph ontology.
